                                 Preface

Training Circular (TC) 7-0.4, Training and Evaluation Outlines (T&EOs), provides Army trainers and leaders
with a fundamental explanation of how to use T&EOs in the planning, preparation, evaluation, and
assessment of task-based training. Users of this TC should familiarize themselves with Army training
doctrine as outlined in ADP 7-0 and FM 7-0. Additionally, they should utilize web-based resources to locate
the appropriate T&EOs to support their organization's training objectives.

The principal audience for TC 7-0.4 is all members of the profession of arms. Commanders and staffs of
Army headquarters serving as joint task force or multinational headquarters must also refer to applicable joint
or multinational doctrine concerning the range of military operations and joint or multinational forces.
Trainers and educators throughout the Army also use this publication.

Commanders, staffs, and subordinates ensure that their decisions and actions comply with applicable United
States, international, and, in some cases, host-nation laws and regulations. Commanders at all levels ensure
that their Soldiers operate in accordance with the law of war and the rules of engagement. (See FM 6-27.)

TC 7-0.4 uses joint terms where applicable. Selected joint and Army terms and definitions appear in both the
glossary and the text. For definitions shown in the text, the term is italicized, and the number of the proponent
publication follows the definition.

TC 7-0.4 applies to the Active Army, Army National Guard/Army National Guard of the United States and
United States Army Reserve unless otherwise stated.

The proponent of TC 7-0.4 is the United States Army Combined Arms Center. The preparing agency is the
Training Management Directorate (TMD) of the United States Army Combined Arms Center – Training
(CAC-T). Send comments and recommendations on DA Form 2028 (Recommended Changes to Publications

or email trainingdoctrine@army.mil.

                              Introduction

The U.S. Army is a standards-based organization that establishes and adheres to clear, uniform standards for
everything from individual performance to collective operations. These standards ensure consistency,
accountability, and effectiveness across all levels of the force.

The Army publishes extensive doctrine, manuals, and Training and Evaluation Outlines (T&EOs) to define
expectations for tasks, skills, and procedures. These tools guide Soldiers and units in achieving proficiency
and readiness.

Training Circular (TC) 7-0.4, Training and Evaluation Outlines, provides detailed descriptions of T&EO
components and their use. A T&EO is a summary document that outlines performance and proficiency
standards for individual and collective tasks. It includes information on task training performance steps,
resource requirements, doctrine references, related tasks, and evaluation measures. T&EOs are essential
training tools that support successful task execution. They are developed and published by the proponent.

This TC focuses on T&EOs for collective tasks and applies to all Army leaders, Soldiers, and the three
components of the Total Army: the Regular Army, the Army National Guard, and the Army Reserve.

TC 7-0.4 contains two chapters:

Chapter 1. A T&EO is a standardized document used to outline performance and proficiency standards for
individual and collective tasks based on Army doctrine. It provides critical guidance for training and
evaluating soldiers, focusing on three key elements: task (the specific action to be performed), conditions
(the circumstances under which the task is executed), and standards (the benchmarks for successful task
completion). T&EOs are accessible through platforms like the Army Training Network (ATN), Digital
Training Management System (DTMS), and Central Army Registry (CAR).

Chapter 2. Army T&EOs provide a structured framework for training and evaluating tasks. They are
designed to ensure standardized training by defining consistent conditions, standards, and performance steps
for individual and collective tasks. T&EOs guide evaluations through clear criteria for assessing task
proficiency, support resource planning by outlining equipment and facilities needed for task execution and
facilitate feedback by providing a basis for commanders to assess readiness and deliver constructive insights.

TC 7-0.2 contains one appendix:

Appendix A. Individual Tasks. Individual tasks, while slightly different from collective tasks, follow the
same structure by emphasizing tasks, conditions, and standards necessary for successful execution. Non-
Commissioned Officers (NCOs) play a central role in selecting, sequencing, and planning individual tasks
and drills to ensure Soldiers are trained on essential unit-critical tasks that contribute to mission success.

                                                 Chapter 1

       Introduction to Training and Evaluation Outlines

OVERVIEW

     1-1. A Training and Evaluation Outline (T&EO) is a summary document that outlines performance and
     proficiency standards for both individual and collective tasks. Developed, approved, and published by
     responsible proponents, based on Army doctrine, T&EOs provide essential information for training and
     evaluating task proficiency. They emphasize the task, conditions, and standards required for successful
     task execution. By focusing on these elements, T&EOs ensure that Army training is both goal-oriented and
     standardized. This is an explanation of each element:

          • Task: A clearly defined action to be executed, ensuring full understanding of what needs to be
                achieved.

          • Conditions: These refer to the specific circumstances and variables under which a task is performed,
                establishing the operational environment for execution. Conditions define the context by including
                factors that influence performance, such as the type of enemy force, available equipment, resources,
                or existing limitations. They also encompass the environment or scenario in which the task will take
                place.

          • Standards: These are the benchmarks for success, specifying the level of performance required to
                meet the task objectives.

SOURCES

     1-2. T&EOs are available through the following web-based resources. For detailed instructions on locating
     and utilizing T&EOs, refer to the unit training management (UTM) interactive training doctrine page on the
     Army Training Network (ATN): https://atn.army.mil/interactive-training-doctrine.

          • ATN T&EO Task Search Tool.
          • Combined Arms Training Strategies (CATS) specifically by unit in the Unit Task List (UTL) and

                Task Sets.
          • Mission Essential Task List (METL) viewer tab on ATN home page.
          • Digital Training Management System (DTMS).
          • Central Army Registry (CAR).

     1-3. By using a common access card (CAC), users can access T&EOs through the ATN without requiring
     additional privileges to view, download, and print them. Alternatively, users can access T&EOs from a
     personal device or tablet by creating a username and password through the Identity, Credential, and Access
     Management (ICAM) portal (https://icamportal.us.army.mil/welcome). However, T&EOs not designated
     for unlimited distribution by the proponent may be unavailable via this method. Additionally, T&EOs
     obtained from non-government-approved sources should not be used.

PURPOSE

    1-4. By systematically breaking down tasks, T&EOs empower leaders and trainers to effectively plan,

     design, execute, and evaluate training sessions. This process ensures that Soldiers are thoroughly prepared
     for real-world missions. T&EOs serve three primary purposes:

          • Standardization—Prepare and practice: T&EOs ensure consistency in training and evaluation
                procedures across units and organizations. Trainers use them to prepare for and practice task
                execution, helping to educate and familiarize Soldiers with the task's requirements and standards.
                As Soldiers practice, task standards form the foundation for developing unit-specific tactics and
                techniques to execute the task effectively during operations.

          • Evaluate training. As tasks are performed during training events, trainers and evaluators use
                T&EOs to observe and evaluate task proficiency. When complete, T&EOs provide an important
                part of feedback that unit commanders review to consider whether the unit can or cannot meet task
                requirements.

PREPARE AND PRACTICE TASKS

     1-5. Preparation through Study: Prior to training, leaders study T&EOs to familiarize themselves and their
     Soldiers with task conditions, standards, and performance steps and measures. Prepared leaders and trainers
     ensure that units understand how to perform tasks successfully. This preparation enables leaders and Soldiers
     to consider and rehearse the best ways to train and execute tasks effectively, achieving optimal results.

             Note: In preparation for training, commanders can use the T&EO to facilitate the "Train and
             Certify Leaders" process. Utilizing T&EOs during evaluator and leader rehearsals ensures that
             observers, trainers, safeties, and other personnel are adequately trained on the expected standards
             prior to the event.

     1-6. Resource Identification: Reviewing task T&EOs enables leaders to identify the resources their unit may
     require for task execution. These resources include training aids, devices, simulators, and simulations
     (TADSS), equipment, training areas, and additional supporting assets. Cross-referencing T&EOs with the
     unit's CATS and related training events can provide more detailed information about the types and quantities
     of resources necessary for effective training.

     1-7. Building Proficiency through Practice: Practicing task execution is an essential component of training.
     It enables Soldiers and units to progressively achieve task standards through repeated iterations. This iterative
     practice builds confidence and ensures that tasks can be performed consistently and reliably, even in varied
     and challenging conditions.

EVALUATE TRAINING

     1-8. Primary trainers and evaluators rely on T&EOs to observe and assess task performance. They provide
     detailed instructions and criteria for effectively conducting and evaluating tasks. Evaluators also use T&EOs
     to plan and prepare their observation strategies. They identify the task standards required for successful task
     execution and evaluate performance based on the objective task criteria matrix, as well as specific
     performance steps and measures. Key aspects of using T&EO’s for evaluation:

          • Develop a plan for evaluation before training execution. FM 7-0 and TC 7-0.1 provide detailed
                information on developing an evaluation plan.

          • The unit commander is responsible for ensuring all trainers and evaluators are qualified to perform
                the tasks.

          • Document the evaluation for future use and make available to unit commanders for use in their
                assessment.

             Note: T&EO documents are completed and verified by evaluators before, during, and after training
             events. At the conclusion of training, final T&EOs are reviewed and submitted for commanders’
             approval. However, units training in field conditions may face challenges like unfavorable weather
             or limited visibility, which can hinder documentation on traditional loose-leaf paper. To address
             these challenges, leaders are encouraged to design and implement durable, field-expedient T&EO
             materials. Techniques such as increasing font size, highlighting key text, and using lamination or
             weatherproofing help evaluators efficiently capture notes despite harsh conditions. Once
             conditions improve, leaders ensure that captured data is transcribed onto official T&EO forms.

     1-9. Commanders refer to evaluator-completed T&EOs as well as other sources of feedback to make an
     informed and objective assessment of the unit’s ability to perform tasks to the standard. In doing so, the
     evaluator’s T&EO provides a primary record of how the task was performed. The commander also considers

                                                 Chapter 2

       Components of Training and Evaluation Outlines

OVERVIEW

     2-1. The basic concept of Army T&EOs revolves around providing a structured framework for training and
     evaluating tasks. T&EOs are designed to:

          • Standardize Training: They ensure consistency by defining clear conditions, standards, and
                performance steps and measures for individual and collective tasks.

          • Guide Evaluations: T&EOs include detailed criteria and instructions for observing and assessing
                task performance, helping evaluators determine a measure of task proficiency.

          • Support Resource Planning: They outline the resources required, such as equipment, training aids,
                and facilities and considerations requirements for performing the task in a specified operational
                environment to effectively execute tasks.

          • Facilitate Feedback: T&EOs serve as a foundation for commanders to assess unit readiness and
                provide constructive feedback.

     2-2. This design ensures that training is goal-oriented, measurable, and aligned with mission requirements.

             Note: When developing T&EOs, proponents follow a standard format and design. However, each
             proponent may adjust the task content to align with the unit type, target echelon, specific
             operational environment requirements, and other relevant factors as needed. While this chapter
             outlines the basic components of T&EOs, it is not intended to cover all the unique features specific
             to each proponent's design.

TASK NUMBER

   2-4. A task number serves as a unique identifier for designating either an individual or collective task. The
   numbering system for individual tasks is separate from that of collective tasks (refer to Appendix A for
   examples and codes of individual tasks). For collective tasks, the task number is composed of two numeric
   groups and one alphanumeric group, each separated by hyphens.

   2-5. For example, the task number 17-CO-1094 Conduct an Attack breaks down as follows: The first numeric
   group, "17," represents the school or proponent code. A proponent refers to an Army organization or staff
   with primary responsibility for specific materials or subject matter within its area of expertise (see Table 2-
   1). In this case, "17" indicates an armor task, designating the Maneuver Center of Excellence as the proponent
   responsible for the task.

   2-6. Using the example "17-CO-1094, Conduct an Attack," the second group of letters ("CO") represents the
   echelon code, defining the level of command to which the collective task applies. In this case, "CO" indicates
   that the task is intended for a company-level unit. At the brigade and lower echelons, there are no multi-
   echelon tasks; each echelon has its own distinct set of tasks. If a suitable collective task for a specific echelon
   is unavailable, leaders should identify and select the most relevant collective or individual tasks that align
   with their mission requirements.

   2-7. The school or proponent designates the final four digits of the task number, guaranteeing each task
   remains uniquely identifiable and distinct from others. Refer to Tables 2-1 and 2-2 for additional information

TASK TITLE

     2-8. The task title, assigned by the proponent, serves as the name of the task. It includes a description of the
     task and specifies the echelon it applies to. For instance, task number 07-CO-1256 is titled Conduct an Attack
     by Fire (Company), as designated by the proponent. When citing tasks in training documents, always include
     both the task number and title to ensure the correct task is clearly identifiable.

CONDITIONS

     2-9. A task condition statement describes the specific circumstances under which a task is to be performed.
     This includes details about the operational environment, available resources, any constraints or limitations,
     and the anticipated threat. It may also provide specific information affecting the necessary operational
     variables by echelon. The conditions statement ensures that the task is evaluated in a realistic and
     standardized context.

STANDARDS

     2-10. The task standard statement outlines the criteria used to determine the minimum acceptable proficiency
     for performing a task under operating conditions. Users consult the task criteria matrix to identify these
     minimum standards. The school or proponent is responsible for specifying and updating the matrix to reflect
     the requirements of the task, unit type, and echelon.

Night or Day

     2-18. Proponents will specify the criteria for evaluating training a “T” based on execution during either day
     or night conditions.

Training Environment

     2-19. A training environment is comprised of conditions, supporting resources, and time that enables training
     tasks to be proficient. There are three basic training environments: live, virtual, and constructive (L/V/C).
     With unlimited time and resources, units execute realistic training in a live environment. The realities of
     limited training time and resources dictate that commanders use creative and innovative means and resources
     to train in other-than-live training environments, or in a combination of all three environments.

     2-20. Live training refers to exercises conducted in field conditions utilizing tactical equipment. It involves
     real individuals operating actual systems in physical, hands-on scenarios, such as maneuvering troops and
     employing equipment in realistic operational settings. Units can integrate TADSS to further enhance the
     effectiveness of live training.

     2-21. Virtual training involves real people operating simulated or actual systems. Units use virtual training
     to exercise motor control, decision-making, and communication skills. Gaming is a subset of the virtual
     training environment and uses gaming technologies to create capabilities to help train individuals and small
     units.

     2-22. Constructive training is a combination of live and virtual environments. It uses computer models and
     simulations to exercise command and staff functions. It involves real people interacting with simulated units
     and operating simulated systems. Constructive training can be conducted by units from platoon through
     echelons above corps. For a comprehensive explanation of each training environment, refer to FM 7-0.

EXECUTE

     2-23. The "Execute" section specifies elements that establish criteria for the leaders and soldiers involved,
     including whether an EXEVAL was resourced and executed, as well as a determination of performance
     measures completed. It includes the following columns:

          • Percent leaders present at training/required.
          • Percent (Soldiers) present at training/required.
          • External evaluation.
          • Performance measures (% of total number of measures repaired for each rating).
          • Critical performance measures (all required for T).
          • Leader performance measures (% of total number of measures repaired for each rating).

Percent of Leaders Present

     2-24. The evaluator records the percentage of unit leaders present in leadership positions during training.
     Unit leaders are defined as those serving in leadership roles. If a staff sergeant is permanently assigned as a
     platoon sergeant, that position is included in the count. The task performance summary matrix, "% Leaders
     Present at Training/Required," compares the number of specific leaders present for training against the total
     number of leaders described for the task in the standards section of the T&EO. The total number of required
     leaders is determined from the relevant column of the table of organization and equipment (TOE), modified
     table of organization and equipment (MTOE), or table of distribution and allowance (TDA). For example, if
     a T&EO identifies platoon leaders, platoon sergeants, and squad leaders as the required leaders for a
     collective task, the TOE, MTOE, or TDA specifies the number of platoons and squads within the unit.

             Note: In the “percent of leaders present” and the “percent of Soldiers present” columns, generally
             the word used is “required.” Some T&EO’s use the word “authorized.” In both instances, the intent
             is to measure against the designated strength in the TOE, MTOE or TDA.

External Evaluation

     2-26. To achieve a “T” proficiency rating in the task, an external evaluation (EXEVAL) is required. An
     EXEVAL consists of someone outside of the organization observing the task performance and providing
     feedback. For example, the 1st platoon leader and platoon sergeant observes the 2nd platoon in the
     performance of the task. The commander two echelons above the unit specifies which tasks receive an
     EXEVAL and is responsible for resourcing the evaluation. Commanders are responsible for ensuring all
     evaluators are trained and fully understand the task performance criteria. See FM 7-0 for detailed information
     on EXEVAL planning.

Performance Measures

     2-27. Performance steps are actions that compose or inform the completion of a task. Performance is recorded
     as GO, NO-GO, or N/A (not applicable if the evaluator did not witness the performance step). Numbered
     steps are always measured while sub steps are not, except for ‘critical’ and ‘leader’ sub steps.

Critical Performance Measures

     2-28. The proponent identifies the critical performance steps and indicates them on the T&EO with a [+] next
     to the step. All critical performance measures must achieve a GO to achieve a task proficiency rating of “T”.

Leader Performance Measures

     2-29. The proponent identifies the leader performance steps and indicates these on the T&EO with a [*] next
     to the step. The percentage is determined by the total number of leader tasks designated. Leader performance
     steps must achieve a GO in 80% of these to achieve a task proficiency rating of “T”. “P” ratings are
     determined if 60-79% of the leader steps/measures are evaluated as a GO.

EVALUATE

     2-30. The "Evaluate" section allows the primary observer/evaluator to determine training proficiency,
     followed by the commander’s assessment. It includes the following columns:

          • Evaluator’s observed task proficiency rating.
          • Commanders’ assessment.

             Note: Army collective tasks have standards outlined in the T&EO. Generally established in the
             standards paragraph and further shaped through the “Objective Task Evaluation Criteria Matrix”
             and the observation of the execution of performance steps and measures. This section allows
             evaluators to annotate their observed performance proficiency ratings: T (Trained), P
             (Practiced), and U (Untrained).

     actions that constitute or inform the completion of a task. Performance is evaluated as GO, NO-GO, or N/A

             can decide if all sub steps will receive a GO/NO-GO check or if the evaluator will only use the
             sub steps to inform the completion of the numbered step.

     2-38. If the proponent labels a performance substep as critical or leader (annotated by a ‘+’, or *), then the
     parent numbered step is categorized as a critical performance step or leader performance step.

Supporting Collective Task(s)

     2-42. Supporting collective tasks (SCTs) are designed to improve proficiency in other collective tasks, such
     as Mission Essential Tasks (METs) or battle tasks. These tasks complement a unit's primary training
     objectives by focusing on specific skills or actions essential to the success of the supported tasks. Each SCT
     is accompanied by its own T&EO. Certain performance steps may require an SCT, and proponents might
     include suggested SCTs within the performance steps. During training planning, trainers and commanders
     evaluate the unit's capability to execute the required steps. If necessary, an SCT can be incorporated into the

OPFOR Task(s)

     2-43. OPFOR tasks replicate the tactics, techniques, and procedures of potential adversaries in military
     training exercises. These tasks are designed to create realistic and challenging scenarios to prepare training
     units for real-world operations. When live training is planned, the proponent may recommend tasks to support
     the commander’s designated training objective. The goal is to provide a dynamic and adaptive adversary,
     enhancing the realism and effectiveness of training.

Supporting Individual Task(s)

     2-44. Supporting individual tasks are specific tasks that help Soldiers develop the skills and knowledge
     necessary to perform their duties effectively. These tasks are designed to complement and enhance the
     proficiency of individual Soldiers in their roles, often aligning with collective tasks or mission-essential tasks.
     Each supporting individual task has its own T&EO, which provides the task, conditions, standards, and
     performance measures specific to that task. Noncommissioned officers (NCOs) are responsible for
     identifying and prioritizing the necessary individual tasks in support of the commander’s identified collective
     training in the unit’s training plan. The selected individual tasks will often support multiple other collective
     tasks such as METs and battle tasks.

Supporting Drill(s)

     2-45. An Army drill is a collective action executed by a unit without the need for deliberate decision-making.
     These drills are pre-planned and practiced responses to common combat situations, designed to ensure swift
     and coordinated actions under pressure. Battle drills focus on simple, standardized procedures that Soldiers
     and units can perform instinctively during chaotic or high-stress scenarios. When appropriate, the proponent
     will recommend drills that support the training and execution of the collective tasks.

                       Appendix A

                Individual Tasks

OVERVIEW

     A-1. Individual tasks slightly differ from collective tasks but share the same overall structure. They
     emphasize the task, conditions, and standards necessary for successful execution. Users can typically rely on
     the content provided in this TC to locate and analyze individual tasks for training purposes.

     A-2. NCOs select and sequence the individual tasks and drills to focus Soldier training. This ensures the
     organization trains on its most important tasks down to the individual Soldier. NCOs are responsible to plan
     the individual training that Soldiers need to become warriors and contribute to the execution of the unit’s
     mission. Soldier Training Publications (STP), also known as Soldier’s Manuals (SMs), provide the critical
     individual tasks for Soldiers to master. The unit’s NCO leadership use the individual tasks in SMs to train
     their Soldiers and evaluate each Soldier’s proficiency for these designated unit-critical tasks.

TYPES

A-3. Army Regulation 350-1, identifies additional HQDA Common Mandatory Training (CMT)
requirements which are applicable to all Soldiers. CMT focuses on topics which are directed by law, federal
statute, or Army Senior Leader guidance. Examples are Sexual Harassment and Assault Response Program
(SHARP), anti-terrorism, and Threat Awareness & Reporting Program (TARP).

A-4.    Other types of tasks:
     •
     •     Army Warrior Training. All Soldiers, regardless of their Military Occupational Specialty (MOS),
           are warriors, first and foremost. STP 21-1-SMCT identifies the individual tasks which are
     •     designated as Army Warrior Tasks. These tasks are a collection of individual Soldier skills known
           to be critical to Soldier survival and enhance their individual readiness to fight on the battlefield.
     •     Army Warrior Tasks are integrated by NCOs into the unit’s overall training plan and constantly
           evaluated and re-evaluated to ensure their Soldiers remain proficient.

           Military Occupational Skill (MOS) Training. MOS training ensures Soldiers achieve and sustain
           proficiency in their MOS and can fully apply their unique skills during operations. MOS-specific
           STPs are the primary reference for Soldiers and NCOs in supporting self-development, training and
           evaluating MOS proficiency. Additionally, Army proponents publish ICTL for each MOS’ skill
           level to identify those individual tasks which must be mastered by the Soldier during their
           development as Army professionals. These ICTLs identify where each individual task is first trained
           to the Soldier and then how often the task should be re-evaluated to sustain proficiency.

           Individual and crew-served weapons. All Soldiers, regardless of MOS or rank, strive to be experts
           in the lethal and ethical employment of their weapon systems. Training Circular (TC) 3-20.40
           describes how a unit builds their unit training plan (UTP) for individual weapon proficiency. It
           provides Soldiers and NCOs all the information needed to plan, prepare, execute, and assess
           individual weapons training strategies and certifications. Effective weapons training combines live
           and virtual training to prepare and achieve advanced certifications. Supporting TCs and Technical
           Manuals (TM) provide the requisite standards to train Soldiers to employ crew-served weapons.

           Holistic health and fitness (known as H2F) includes mental fitness and physical training. H2F is
           the foundation of unit readiness. Field Manual 7-22 establishes the Army’s doctrine for the readiness
           training of Soldiers. It provides Soldiers and NCOs with the individualized training and testing
           procedures that builds peak performance in all Soldiers.

                                         Air Force Doctrine Publication 3-34, Engineer Operations

                                         FOREWORD

Air Force Doctrine Publication (AFDP) 3-34, Engineer Operations, recognizes that from
the beginning of military aviation, engineers have ensured airfields, facilities, and
infrastructure set the conditions for airpower to achieve operational dominance. This
publication also guides the United States Air Force (USAF) Civil Engineer (CE) enterprise
in a new reality where operating in contested, degraded, and operationally limited
environments is a primary concern. In this complex world, installations remain the power
projection platforms (PPP) from which the USAF employs airpower. Engineers are critical
because they build, maintain, and recover those installations. The underlying imperative
for AFDP 3-34 is for Airman to understand how engineers employ wartime and
contingency skills and execute mission-essential tasks in joint all-domain operations to
ensure PPPs can support joint force commander (JFC) objectives.
To effectively organize and employ engineer forces throughout the competition
continuum, AFDP 3-34 describes the principles and best practices of engineer operations
by incorporating the joint functions and tenets of airpower. It communicates how the
USAF's CE enterprise performs key functions, including general engineering, geospatial
engineering, and installation support and services. This publication also discusses how
USAF engineers organize into specialized teams with varying capabilities, such as Prime
Base Engineer Emergency Force (Prime BEEF) and Rapid Engineer Deployable Heavy
Operational Repair Squadron Engineer (RED HORSE). Furthermore, it incorporates
critical engineering capabilities, such as Integrated Base Response and Recovery (IBRR)
and Rapid Airfield Damage Repair (RADR), that are essential to successfully executing
the Agile Combat Employment (ACE) scheme of maneuver.
Finally, AFDP 3-34 highlights critical engineering capabilities that enable air and space
operations in any environment, ultimately contributing to joint operations and enhancing
a JFC's ability to accomplish their assigned mission. To meet this goal, Airmen should be
trained to support operations in a distributed or decentralized manner, execute their
mission when isolated from higher echelons, and be comfortable making decisions while
operating in line with commander’s intent. Therefore, AFDP 3-34 incorporates the
philosophy of mission command with foundational CE doctrine and practice, making it a
cornerstone within the USAF operational doctrine library.

        CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION TO ENGINEER OPERATIONS

                         “Air bases are a determining factor in the success of air operations.
                         The two-legged stool of men and planes would topple over without
                         this equally important third leg.”

                                                                   –General Henry H. “Hap” Arnold

The Civil Engineer (CE) enterprise is the primary provider of civil engineering support for
United States Air Force (USAF) and United States Space Force (USSF) installations. The
CE enterprise consists of a total force mix of active duty USAF, Air Force Reserve (AFR),
Air National Guard (ANG), and civilian engineers who perform installation operations
support, construction projects, and emergency response activities. Total Force USAF
engineers are assigned or attached to numbered, specialized, or institutional units. They
can complete their missions at bare bases, main operating bases, joint bases, forward
operating bases, collocated operating bases, and combat outposts.

FOUNDATIONAL PRINCIPLES OF CIVIL ENGINEERS

Engineers provide a critical capability because the USAF projects airpower from
installations, and engineers build, maintain, and recover those installations. From the
beginning of military aviation in the United States (US) to today, engineers have been
pivotal players, ensuring airfields, facilities, and infrastructure help set the conditions for
operational dominance.

Just as a runway is foundational to air operations, basic foundational beliefs of USAF civil
engineers provide the basis for delivering USAF and USSF installations effectively and
efficiently, using modern principles to maximize contributions to installations and their
diverse mission sets. Engineers have an outward focus to support the warfighter and
ensure their power projection platforms (PPP) are ready for employment by supporting
broader mission-readiness issues and developing and sustaining a total force of civilian,
active-duty, and air reserve engineers.

Engineers are innovative in their problem-solving approach. They seek effective and
efficient solutions to problems across the doctrine, organization, training, materiel,
leadership, education, personnel, facilities, and policy lines of effort. Modernization of CE
capabilities, infrastructure, equipment, and tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTP)
(including chemical, biological, radiological, or nuclear [CBRN] defense areas) is essential
to keeping pace with capable adversaries. Infrastructure that provides redundancy,
resiliency, and flexibility is critical to maintaining power projection capabilities in a highly
contested environment. More innovative, more efficient practices reduce waste and
promote the economy of assets. Engineers leverage emerging advances in control
systems to improve energy efficiency and monitoring while eliminating and securing
critical infrastructure from adversary cyberspace attacks. Modernization enables the
USAF to outpace its competitors through industry technological innovations and
increased resiliency.

Engineer operational doctrine provides a guide for effectively organizing and employing
engineer forces across the complexities of the competition-to-conflict continuum. As the
USAF continuously improves its airpower capabilities, its ability to identify the latest best
practices and incorporate new concepts and technologies may revolutionize and shape
future engineering operations and doctrine.

OPERATIONAL EMPLOYMENT IN CONTESTED ENVIRONMENTS

Innovative Airmen are integral to improving and developing processes and practices,
succeeding as they connect, share, and learn. Engineer operations in a contested
environment against a peer require the air component to be adaptive, resilient, and agile
in its deployment, employment, and leadership philosophies. Engineer teams and
leadership typically need to employ core aspects of Mission Command by exercising
disciplined initiative and accepting prudent risk to make decisions on critical, time-
sensitive activities, such as rapid airfield repair, and to adapt engineering techniques.

Installations are PPP-built, maintained, and recovered by ready engineers, trained and
equipped to carry out combat support operations within their assigned units of action.
Engineers train at home station, combat support training ranges, exercise deployments,
and Silver Flag sites on pre-identified engineering operations. This training permits the
rapid employment of Prime Base Engineer Emergency Force (Prime BEEF), Emergency
Services, and Rapid Engineer Deployable Heavy Operational Repair Squadron Engineer
(RED HORSE) Squadron (RHS) engineers to support global military operations.
Additionally, by incorporating asset management and lifecycle practices into PPP, risks
to the installation mission are reduced or mitigated through modernization. Finally, USAF
engineers serve as a force multiplier in executing general engineering operations.1

USAF CE enhances a joint force commander's (JFC’s) ability to accomplish the mission
and enable joint operations. The primary task for USAF engineers is to enable USAF and
USSF missions. USAF engineers are trained and equipped with organic capabilities to
support all aspects of projecting air and space power. Secondarily, they also provide
general engineering support for other Service requirements. USAF engineers also
conduct certain combat engineer functions in contingency and warfighting environments,
both on the installation and off.

Engineers prepare for contingencies by training as units, applying lessons learned, and
exercising their capabilities to deploy qualified engineer forces to rapidly establish
airbases as PPP, repair battle-damaged facilities and infrastructure, and recover
installations from man-made and natural disasters. A ready Airman engineer is prepared
to employ wartime and contingency skills to execute mission-essential tasks and mission
capabilities in all military operations, regardless of the location or type of military
installation. The USAF sees these locations or installations in the context of an airfield,
described as an area prepared for the takeoff, landing, and accommodation of aircraft
(including any buildings, installations, and equipment required).

Each Service possesses engineering forces that provide a capability to establish and
maintain bases. However, each of those Services maintain core engineering capabilities
stemming from traditional roles to meet specific operational needs. USAF civil engineers
can open airfields once secured by ground or host nation (HN) forces. In highly contested
and austere locations, engineer leadership should be prepared to accept higher levels of
risk and allow USAF Civil Engineer teams to conduct airfield opening activities in locations
without ground force security. USAF engineers have expertise in support of the maneuver
of conventional and unconventional USAF warfighters.

Furthermore, USAF Civil Engineers enable airpower and space power operations in and
through any environment through integrated base response and recovery (IBRR).
Recovery activities directly contribute to the joint force’s ability to counter adversary kill
chains and operations by mitigating the effects of adversary attacks.2 USAF engineers
assist air and ground maneuver operations by constructing expeditionary landing
surfaces and reinforcing structures to mitigate the impacts of direct and indirect weapons.

USAF civil engineers possess broad engineering skills to plan, build, maintain, operate,
protect, recover, sustain, and close military facilities and infrastructure. These capabilities
support combatant commanders (CCDR) in both peacetime and contingency operations.
Through hands-on experience at home stations and deployments, combined with
specialized training in technical tasks, combat skills, career education, and contingency
equipment, USAF engineers are fully prepared to manage contingency bases, serve on
Service or joint staffs, and perform theater-level engineering duties in any operational
environment worldwide. The USAF embeds military engineers, civilians, and contractors
into the home station workforce. Providing general engineering, geospatial engineering,
installation support and service, emergency services, engineering logistics services, and
partnership building capabilities in this manner is a force multiplier. Mixing military (active
duty and AFR), civilian, and contractor personnel creates an environment to leverage the
technical expertise and experience of long-term civilians to train military engineer forces,
while simultaneously accomplishing the home station mission, which includes projecting
airpower into multiple theaters simultaneously as well as ensuring readiness of the
installation’s primary weapon systems.

ORGANIZATION AND CORE OPERATIONAL PILLARS

Typically, USAF operational engineering forces are organized into flights or squadrons,
with several instances of groups. Engineer forces are also assigned to USAF institutional
organizations, such as above wing-level staffs, the Air Force Installation and Mission
Support Center, the Air Force Civil Engineer Center (AFCEC), and the Air Force Institute
of Technology.
AFCEC’s Civil Engineer Capability Requirements, Research and Development, and
Acquisition Division within the Readiness Directorate takes input from multiple sources to
drive innovation, research, and development of new capabilities.
Engineer capabilities enable operational and tactical maneuvers by supporting actions
necessary for the commander to meet mission objectives. CE core operational pillars,
structured to meet JFC and other operational requirements, are:
 Prime BEEF.
 RED HORSE.
 Explosive ordnance disposal (EOD).
 Fire and emergency services.
 Emergency management (EM), CBRN specialist teams.
 Other specialized teams.
 Staff augmentation teams (S-Teams).
Civil engineers participate in training activities that support base and homeland
operations, major commands, numbered air forces, and their subordinate wings. The CE
leadership structure provides standardized guidance, training, equipment, and
procedures through a corporate process. While at home station, civil engineers establish
main operating bases to establish resilience against potential attacks by capable
adversaries. Preparation and training at main operating bases are designed to protect
and recover the bases and PPP.

INSTALLATION SUPPORT AND SERVICES

Engineers provide installation services to support military forces, including activities
essential for effective real property lifecycle management. This capability focuses on
managing real property facilities and infrastructure in the US and bases in other CCDR
areas of responsibility while providing protection, safety, security, and sustainability for
personnel and mission-critical built and natural assets. Installation support activities take
place before, after, or concurrently with general engineering activities. These actions
apply across military operations and include real property life cycle management,
operations, emergency services, and related activities.

REAL PROPERTY LIFE CYCLE MANAGEMENT

Engineers provide for planning, acquisition, recapitalization, disposal, and economic
adjustment activities of real property assets to meet operational requirements.

 Planning: With the mission owners, engineers identify mission priorities and the most
     pressing needs for the Department of the Air Force (DAF) or joint installations and
     then translate plans into base infrastructure through real estate, basing, and facility
     options. Effective planners understand the requirements. They look to the future
     created by high-technology weapons systems and new operational concepts,
     including cybersecurity concerns. Planning:

      Focuses on base layout, environmental factors, infrastructure, and subsystems,
           ensuring all requirements meet theater construction standards.

      Provides a logical progression of facilities and infrastructure development that
           fits with provisions for long-range growth or mission realignment and issues such
           as flexibility, redundancy, resilience, and survivability.

      Maintains awareness of base support plans to identify resources and shortfalls
           required to support specific operation and concept plans.

      Includes research, development, and funding needed to provide mission-capable
           facilities.

      Takes into consideration force protection (FP) concerns wherever bases are to
           be developed.

 Acquisition: Engineers translate base plans into infrastructure through a range of
     real estate, basing, and facility options. Engineers work with contracting and legal
     functions to purchase, lease, construct, or gain installation assets. An acquisition can
     include land, natural resources, buildings, structures, portable facilities, airfields,
     roads, installed equipment, and all interests in the property.

 Recapitalization: Using asset management principles, engineers restore,
     modernize, and replace installation assets to meet mission requirements and comply
     with construction standards and statutory/regulatory requirements.

OPERATIONS

Continuing the real property cycle, engineers bridge the gap between new facilities and
those at the end of their life cycle by applying smart and secure asset management
principles to operate and sustain facilities and infrastructure.

 Installation Operations: Engineers operate and maintain the infrastructure to
     support missions. The requirement is to balance mission effectiveness versus the
     efficient performance of infrastructure supporting base activities. They provide
     functional real property installation assets, including utilities such as energy, water,
     and wastewater disposal, and contract and real property management, pollution
     prevention, and other essential services.

 Sustainment: Regular surveys of base layout, facilities, infrastructure, and
     equipment are conducted to enhance FP and strengthen the base’s combat
     capability. Environmental and energy conservation and reduction plans are
     developed to protect the population's health, preserve the environment, and reduce
     waste. These include natural and cultural resources through practical assessment,
     maintenance, and repair of current assets and planning for future missions.
     Maintenance requirements are prioritized based on operational need, resources,
     cost, timing, energy efficiency, reliability, maintainability, environmental impacts,
     safety, and quality of life.

OTHER RELATED ACTIVITIES

Engineers provide additional installation services at contingency locations and advance
the CCDR’s campaign objectives. These tasks are aligned with other joint functions.

 Contingency Base Services: These are engineering activities that support logistical
     services. Examples include shelter beddown, water, waste management, hygiene

     major operations where beddown and sustainment support is unavailable, the
     supported command, Service, or agency provides logistical sustainment

     integrate those programs into solutions that help accomplish national security

     military-civilian engagement. Engineers can provide C2 structures while working
     alongside local engineers, US Government (USG) agencies, and nongovernmental
     organizations (NGOs). Engineers support the development of the air component
     commander’s campaign support plan and operations plans designed to shape the

     security cooperation by exchanging knowledge, technology, and personnel. Providing
     engineering and construction expertise to PNs assists an air component
     commander’s, JFC’s, or CCDR's ability to influence and maintain access in critical

     USAF with critical competencies for planning and coordinating theater-level
     engineering activities, including C2, cost effectively.

                       CHAPTER 3: CIVIL ENGINEER TEAMS

As part of an organized force, USAF engineers are organized into teams with varying
capabilities who contribute general engineering, geospatial engineering, and emergency
services to several joint functions including, sustainment and protection. Teams are
generally presented as Prime BEEF (including emergency services) or RED HORSE.

DEPLOYED ORGANIZATIONAL CONCEPT

USAF civil engineers deploy as combat support units to provide general and geospatial
engineering capabilities, protection, response, recovery, and sustainment across the
competition continuum. Agile Combat Employment (ACE) is the USAF’s scheme of
maneuver designed to increase base survivability while generating combat power.4 The
unique capabilities USAF civil engineers present to the joint force and air component
leaders are a critical enabler to the USAF’s ACE plans.

Prime BEEF organizations rapidly respond worldwide to offer a wide range of engineering
support to establish, operate, sustain, protect, recover, and reconstitute installations.

RED HORSE is a self-sufficient, mobile heavy construction unit capable of rapid response
and independent operations in a contested or terrorist threat environment. They provide
heavy repair and construction capabilities that exceed Prime BEEF's capabilities.

Prime BEEF and RED HORSE force deployments are executed through the joint
deployment system and conducted using guidance issued by Headquarters USAF.
During deployments, CE forces are part of an air component unit of action and follow the
command relationships that affect all Air Force forces. Most deployed Prime BEEF
engineer units currently support an air expeditionary wing construct. Engineers may be
placed under the tactical control of a joint force engineer command structure if established
by the JFC. The air component commander, through the appropriate organizational
structure, normally retains operational control of USAF CE units. Tactical control of CE
elements, especially in ACE environments and in support of Distributed Control and
Decentralized Execution C2 philosophies, can be delegated to leadership levels best
suited to understand the needs of supported operational locations and to make informed,
timely decisions to achieve mission objectives.

Organizing Prime BEEF and RED HORSE under a single USAF engineering commander
is an alternate operational structure to support air component commander requirements.
Under this structure, limited theater-wide engineering forces can be leveraged to
prioritize, while mass engineering capabilities are deployed at the right time and place to
meet demands. Maintaining unit integrity should be considered during design and
planning. Unit integrity enables engineers to train and deploy together, providing the air
component commander with an integrated mission-ready team. Additionally, contingency
plans should be established to ensure CE capabilities remain responsive to air
component units of action should the theater-level C2 structures become unavailable or
unable to effectively understand and respond to the needs of lower echelon units.

                                             Counter Kill Chain

    Advancements in adversary anti-access, area denial capabilities greatly expand
    the range and lethality of adversary kill chains. These enhanced lethal kill chains
    limit friendly freedom of action and raise risk to mission while making achievement
    of the joint force commander’s objectives more difficult. The need to ensure
    friendly movement and maneuver and achieve friendly effects in the operational
    environment has elevated the importance to counter adversary kill chains.

    To attack friendly forces, an adversary must complete a sequence of actions to
    implement their kill chain. They detect friendly forces, communicate via command
    and control networks, decide on engagement options, employ fires, and then
    accomplish post-attack assessments. Engineers help counter adversary kill
    chains by enabling sortie generation, maneuver operations, and other
    ACE-related activities through passive defense, hardening, and recovery
    operations. Engineer support to deception activities, such as camouflage,
    concealment, and decoys (CDD), tactical deception (TAC-D), and deception in
    support of operational security (DISO), serves to complicate adversary
    intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance and targeting (ISRT) processes and
    post-attack damage assessments. Hardening critical infrastructure degrades the
    effects of enemy weapons, while recovery operations, such as rapid airfield
    damage repair, can decrease the effectiveness of adversary attacks.

Engineers seek opportunities to improve RADR processes as adversaries continue to
advance their A2/AD. Equipment, materials, and TTPs provide effective and robust crater
repair capabilities that maximize available aircraft sortie generation while minimizing

CHAPTER 5: OPERATIONS ACROSS THE COMPETITION CONTINUUM

Engineers are critical to military operations, applying their expertise and critical thinking
across the entire competition continuum, from initial theater setup to post-conflict
transition. Their planning, execution, and assessment functions are tailored to the specific
demands of each operation.

SETTING THE THEATER

Engineering operations are fundamental to the joint force's ability to set the theater. They
establish the necessary physical, logistical, and infrastructure conditions required to
enable freedom of action, sustainment, and operational reach. Through deliberate
planning, engineers shape the environment to support the rapid deployment, reception,
staging, onward movement, and integration (RSOI) of forces. Engineers are active in all
phases, from setting and shaping the theater to returning control to civil authority post-
conflict.

CORE ENGINEERING CAPABILITIES FOR SETTING THE THEATER

Engineers contribute decisively to setting the theater by transforming the operational
environment to support joint force objectives. Key activities include:

 Infrastructure Development and Repair: Constructing, expanding, and maintaining
     critical facilities such as ports, airfields, roads, and base camps. These efforts ensure
     the joint force can project power, receive reinforcements, and sustain operations
     across extended lines of communication.

 Mobility and Access: Enhancing maneuverability by improving routes, clearing
     obstacles, and bridging gaps, which maintains operational tempo and enables forces
     to move freely.

 Protection and Survivability: Providing FP through the construction of defensive
     positions, protective barriers, and hardened facilities to safeguard personnel,
     equipment, and critical nodes against environmental hazards and enemy action.

 Sustainment Enablement: Establishing essential life-support infrastructure,
     including power, water, and waste management systems, allowing forces to endure
     and operate effectively in austere environments.

 Theater Security Cooperation: Engaging in projects that strengthen alliances and
     build HN capacity, fostering access, legitimacy, and stability.

In essence, engineering operations are decisive contributors, not just enablers,
transforming the operational environment to support joint force objectives and ensuring
commanders have the freedom to act, sustain, and prevail.

• 53. GENERAL.—The 90-mm antiaircraft artillery gun may be
use% effectively against terrestrial and water-borne targets.
For this purpose direct-tire telescopic sights have been provided
for firing at ranges up to 3,500 yards, the minimum range for
director control.

• 54, DESCRIPTION.—a. Mount*.—Two direct-fire sights are
mounted on the 90-mm gun. Telescope mount M28 with elbow
telescope M24 is used for aiming the gun in azimuth. It is
mounted on the top carnage above the azimuth handwheels
iind rotates with the gun in azimuth only (fig. 36). Telescope
mount M29 supporting elbow telescope M26 is used for aiming
the gun in range. It is mounted on the side of the cradle in
front of the right trunnion: it moves with the gun in azimuth
as well as in elevation (fig. 37).

   &. Telescopes.—Except for their reticle patterns, the two
elbow telescopes, M24 for azimuth and M26 for range, are sim
ilar. Each is a three-power, erect image instrument with a
field of view of 13°20', or %7 of a full circle.

   (1) Azimuth reticle.—The reticle of the azimuth telescope
has a single horizontal scale marked in mils lead (fig. 38). The
minimum graduation is 5 mils and the numbered graduations
are multiples of 10.

   (2) Range reticle.—Across the bottom half of the range
telescope's reticle are seven horizontal range lines for aiming

 only when both the azimuth setter and the range setter signify

    (2) Tracking.—Manual operation of the gun is required while
 using the direct-fire sights. The azimuth setter observes through
 his telescope with his left eye, following the target by turning
the azimuth handwheel. If necessary, he simultaneously twists
the telescope's elevation knob with his left hand to keep the
target within the limits of the reticle. The elevation setter
observes through his telescope with his right eye, following
the target in range by turning the elevation handwheels.

   6. Lateral leads.—A knowledge of initial leads is necessary
for the azimuth setter. Although terrestrial and water-borne
targets are limited to two dimensions in maneuvering rather
than the airplane's three, they will appear abruptly and action
against them will be rapid. Therefore, initial fire must be
accurate. At ranges up to 1,000 yards, time of flight of the
round may be disregarded in estimating the lead; the size and
speed of the target will be the variable factors. A close-in
target, crossing the line of sight at any angle, may safely
be given a lead of anywhere from its forward edge to twice
its length, depending upon its size and speed. If the burst
or splash can be seen by the azimuth setter, he may apply
corrections on the following shots. At a target following a
consistent course, the second shot may be aimed by moving the
target along the azimuth telescope's reticle line to the point
on the reticle line at which the burst or splash was seen.

   c. Ranges.—An ability to estimate ranges is essential for
the range (elevation) setter. If time allows, landmarks may
be spotted as reference marks or datum points, and ranges to
them measured by a map scale, by survey, or by the height
finder. The successive bursts or splashes of the first few rounds,
observed by the setter or the spotter, are indices to be used in

closing in on the target.
  d. Adjustment of fire.—It may be possible to use the height-

finder to track the target in range, corrections being telephoned

by the range spotter to the range setter on the gun. In addition,
a lateral spotter, provided with a battery commander's tele
scope, may telephone corrections to the azimuth setter. How
ever, for practical purposes this manual considers only the
essential spotters (par. 56a(l)) standing off the platform out
side the effect area of muzzle blast and smoke. These men

• 57. AMMUNITION.—a. Types.—A small proportion of armor-
piercing shot (AP) M77 Is provided for fire against armored
targets. In addition, the regular high explosive shell (HE)
M71 is available.

   b. Targets for each type.—Against thinly-shelled targets,
such as motor torpedo boats, unarmored vehicles, or personnel
carriers, HE can be employed effectively. However, most
armored targets—tanks, armored vehicles, fortifications,
armored barages, etc.—will call for AP.

   c. Observing fire.—The technique of direct-sight firing differs
from that of director-controlled firing. In the latter the spotters
at the director telescopes track the target directly on the cross
hairs. In the former the setters at the gun telescopes not only
sight on the target to give it proper leads, but also try to
observe the result of fire to correct those leads. Therefore,
they must see either the bursts or the tracers of rounds in
flight. Time fuzes of HE ammunition to be fired on ground
or water-borne targets will usually be set at "safe"; the burst
ing of the shells on impact should be apparent to the azimuth
and the elevation setters and their respective assistant spotters.
AP ammunition for the 90-mm will be provided with a tracer
element, so that the setters and spotters may more readily
observe the result of fire.

• 58. CARE AND MAINTENANCE.—a. Lubrication.—There are oil
cups on both telescope mounts for the lubrication of the princi
pal bearing surfaces. Oil should be applied as required by serv
ice conditions. Clamp pivots on the telescope brackets should
be oiled occasionally with only a few drops of oil. All excess
oil should be wiped off to prevent any accumulation of dust
and grit.

  b. Cleaning.—The lenses of the telescopes should be cleaned
only with alcohol and a camel's-hair brush. They should be
wiped dry with lens tissue. The rubber eyeshields should be
removed occasionally and washed in warm water.

• 5. EXPLANATION.—The drills are presented in chronologi
cal sequence of action, with explanatory notes. Each action
begins with the numbers or abbreviations signifying the
members of the gun section concerned in that action; these
numbers or abbreviations are at the left margin. Each main
operation performed is separated from other main operations
by _____. In emplacement of the gun and march order,
the front of the mount is the towing or trail end. Left and
right sides of the mount are determined by standing at the
rear and facing the front.

• 6. INSTRUCTIONS.—The drill prescribed herein will be
adhered to strictly, both to prevent injury to materiel and to
produce maximum operating efficiency of the gun section.
Drill is conducted in silence except for commands and reports.
The gun section must be drilled until all reactions to com
mands are automatic, instant, and effective. Errors are cor
rected instantly and on the spot. Drill is conducted at the
fastest pace at which the gun section can operate smoothly.
The ultimate in precision and speed is the goal. Battery
officers must supervise and coach the gun commander to
make sure that the foregoing instructions are carried out and
that the maximum efficiency of the gun section is reached.

 • 40. GENKRAL.—Safety precautions to be observed for train
 ing are prescribed in AR 750-10 and FM 4-118 (when pub
 lished). The more Important safety precautions relating
 to the gun section are summarized in the following para
 graphs. These precautions will be followed as closely as the
 situation permits, even under combat conditions.

 • 41. AMMUNITION.—a. All ammunition at the firing point
 must be placed so as to be protected against explosion in case
 of accident at the gun position. It should be in a dry place
 and protected against direct rays of the sun by a tarpaulin
 or other suitable covering. Erratic shots and dangerously
 high powder pressures may result from overheated ammuni
 tion.

    6. No attempt will be made by battery personnel to dis
 assemble the round or fuze. This is dangerous and is strictly
 prohibited.

    c. If for any reason a round is not fired after the fuze has
 been set, the fuze will be reset at SAFE before putting the
 round away.

    d. In case of a misfire, two attempts are made to flre the
 round before opening the breach and removing the round.

    e. If a live round cannot be extracted in the normal man-
• ner, it is fired, safety precautions permitting. If this is im
 possible, it is removed under the supervision of an officer, a
 rammer being used which bears only on the projectile and
 provides for clearance around the fuze.

 • 42. MATERIEL.—a. In coupling or uncoupling the gun with
 the prime mover, no one should be between the prime mover
and the lifting bar of the trail. Sudden movements of the
 prime mover would crush anyone caught between.

   b. In emplacing the gun it is necessary to compress the
counterpoise springs "before loosening the nuts of the bogie
engaging eye hooks. This must be done to prevent the

 • 44. GENERAL.—a. Tactical situations may arise when, due
 to limitations of time or transportation, it will become im
 possible to evacuate all equipment. In such situations it Is
 imperative that all materiel which cannot be evacuated be
 destroyed to prevent—

    (1) Its capture intact by the enemy.
    (2) Its use by the enemy, if captured, against our own or
 allied troops.
    b. The working principles to be followed are:
    (1) Methods for the destruction of materiel subject to
 capture or abandonment in the combat zone must be ade
 quate, uniform, and easily followed in the field.
    (2) Destruction must be as complete as the available time,
 equipment, and personnel will permit. If thorough destruc
 tion of all parts cannot be completed, the most important
 features of the materiel should be destroyed, and parts es
sential to the operation or use of the materiel and which can
not be easily duplicated, ruined or removed. The same
essential parts must be destroyed on all like units to prevent
the enemy constructing one complete unit from several dam
aged ones by "cannibalization."

      I believe that the Army of today has a much keener appreciation for
the value of observation aircraft than did most of our commanders in
World War II. I used light planes frequently in both training and combat
situations in World War II because they permitted me to observe in a
few minutes what it would have taken days to observe by any other
means. I found invaluable the panorama of the terrain and the disposi-
tions of forces that I could quickly fix in my mind when I could observe
from the air. Then when I later studied the situation on a map, I could
envision much more clearly the exact lay of the land. This was tremend-
ously helpful in the overall direction of the Fifth Army and it was of
particular value in evaluating terrain and its influence on the courses of
action I selected.

      General Mark W. Clark

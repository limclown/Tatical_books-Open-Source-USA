               SECTION I
            INTRODUCTION

1. Purpose and Scope

   a. Cadet drill is authorized only for the United
States Military Academy. It is ideally suited to
occasions when very impressive ceremonies are
desired.

   b. This drill retains the basic structure of
standard drill for simplicity and incorporates the
simpler maneuvers of the old squad drill. It per-
mits the disciplinary value of drill to be exploited
to the maximum because it requires all officers
and men in ranks to remain alert and obey com-
mands with precision and promptness.

2. Training Procedure

   a. The recommended procedure for the corps
of cadets is to learn both the cadet and standard
drills. Cadet drill is more suitable for parades,
reviews, and similar ceremonies, while standard
drill is better suited for street parades, marches,
and field work; and for moving large bodies of
men in restricted areas. The preferred instruc-
tional procedure is to teach cadet drill first. Once
the cadets have become proficient in it, they can
learn standard drill in one or two drill periods.
The basic maneuvers of cadet drill require con-

siderable practice. However, after they have been
mastered, the cadet reaches a state of perfection
in about the same overall time as with standard
drill.

   b. Squad drill is the basis of cadet drill, and
each cadet must learn the movements of each
position in the squad. Squad drill must be mas-
tered completely before platoon and company drill
can be accomplished properly.

6. To Form the Squad for Cadet Drill

   a. The squad leader stands three steps to the
front center of the spot where he wants the squad
to form and commands-FALL IN.

   b. On the command FALL IN, the squad falls
in at attention, three steps from and centered on
the squad leader, in two ranks, in order of height
from right to left. Each man, except the left flank
men, places the heel of his left hand on his left
hip, fingers joined and extended downward, and
his left elbow in line with his body and touching
the arm of the man on his left. Each man looks
to the right and alines himself on the right flank
man. The right flank man of each rank keeps his
head and eyes to the front. When each man is in
line with the men on his right and has obtained
correct interval, he drops his left arm to his side
quietly and turns his head and eyes to the front.
The rear rank takes approximate interval with
40 inches between ranks. File closers fall in 60
inches from the rear rank.

   c. When armed, the men fall in at the order.
After falling in, weapons are inspected on the
commands, INSPECTION, ARMS; PORT, ARMS; and
ORDER, ARMS.

   d. Immediately after forming, the squad should
be given COUNT, OFF, to insure that each member
knows his number.

7. To Count Off

   The command is COUNT, OFF. At the command
of execution, all men except the man on the right
flank of each rank turn their heads and eyes to
the right, and the right flank man of each rank
counts off, ONE. After the man on the right
counts off his number, the man to his left counts
off the next higher number, at the same time turn-
ing his head and eyes to the front. The numbers

8. To Aline the Squad

   a. From a position three steps in front of and
centered on the squad, the squad leader commands

At the command DRESS, each man except the

Each man except the left flank men places the heel
of his left hand on his left hip, fingers joined and
extended downward, and his left elbow in line
with his body and touching the arm of the man
on his left.

   b. The squad leader marches by the most direct
route to a position on line with the first rank and
one step from the man on which the dress is made
and faces down the rank. From this position he
verifies the alinement of the first rank, ordering
men to move forward, or backward as necessary,
calling them by name or number, "Cadet Jones,
forward 2 inches. No. 3 man, back 3 inches." The
squad leader remains at attention, taking short
steps to the right or left as necessary to see down
the squad. He does not use his arms or twist his
body. After alining the first rank, he faces to the
left (right) in marching and, by taking two short
steps, moves to the next rank, halts, faces down
the line, and alines it in the same manner as the
first rank.

9. To Stack Arms

   a. The even-numbered men in the front rank
are the stackmen. To stack arms at a halt with
the squad in line, the command is-STACK, ARMS.

   b. At the command ARMS, each stackman
grasps his weapon with his left hand at the front
hand guard and rests the butt between his feet,
barrel to the front, muzzle outward and, with the
thumb and forefinger of his left hand, raises the
stacking swivel. Each even number of the rear
rank then regrasps his rifle with the right hand
at the balance, carrying it to a horizontal position,
barrel up, and passes it to his file leader (his
stackman). The stackman grasps the weapon at
the front hand guard with his right hand and
throws the butt about 2 feet in front of and 6
inches to the right of his right toe. At the same
time he shifts his right hand to the stacking swivel
and engages it with the rifle between his feet.

   c. The man on the right of the stackman steps
to the left front with his left foot, keeping his
right foot in place. At the same time he bends to
the left front, slides his left hand up his rifle to
manipulate the stacking swivel, grasps the small

10. To Take Arms

   a. Being in line behind the stacks, the command
is: TAKE, ARMS.

   b. At the command ARMS, the extra weapons
are returned by the stackmen. Next, each stack-

detaches it from the stack. Each stackman then
disengages the two remaining rifles, slides his
right hand to the balance of the right rifle, and
passes it to the rear rank man in a vertical posi-
tion. Each man comes to order arms when he has
received his weapon.

11. To Open and Close Ranks

   a. The squad being in line at a halt, the com-
mand to open ranks is-OPEN RANKS, MARCH.
At the command MARCH, the front rank takes
one step forward, halts, and executes dress right
at close interval. The rear rank and file closers
stand fast and execute dress right at close in-
terval.

   b. The squad leader verifies the alinement and
commands-READY, FRONT. This is executed as
described in paragraph 8c.

   c. The squad being at open ranks, the command
to close ranks is-CLOSE RANKS, MARCH. At the
command MARCH, the rear rank and file closers
take one step forward and halt, each man cover-
ing his file leader.

13. To Turn on a Fixed Pivot and March

turn on a fixed pivot and march is-SQUAD RIGHT

tion. On movements from a halt, whether the turn
is to the right or left, the left foot falls on the
odd counts. The sixth count, on which the entire
squad steps off in the new direction, therefore
requires a full step forward with the right foot.

14. To Turn on a Fixed Pivot and Halt

   The squad being in line, the commands to turn
on a fixed pivot and halt are-SQUAD RIGHT
(LEFT), MARCH; SQUAD, HALT. The preparatory
command SQUAD, of SQUAD, HALT, is given im-
mediately after the command of execution,
MARCH. The turn is executed as described in
paragraph 13 except that all men, on arriving on
the new line, mark time until the command of
execution HALT is given. HALT is given on the
fifth count.

15. To Turn About

   a. Being in line, the command to turn about
and march is-SQUAD RIGHT (LEFT) ABOUT,
MARCH.

   b. At the command MARCH, the squad executes
squad right twice. In starting the second squad
right, the foot is planted in the first step on the
sixth count. All execute forward march without

command, the foot being planted in the first step
in the new direction on the eleventh count.

   c. In the rear rank, the third man from the
right marches straight to the front three and one-
half steps, changes direction to the right, and
takes three and one-half steps in the new direction.
The second and first men from the right oblique
to the left, then oblique to the right so as to fol-
low the third man in column as in squad right.
On the ninth count, all three men face to the right
in marching, cover their file leaders, and mark
time. The other number of the rear rank marches
straight to the front four steps, obliques to the
right one step, completes the change of direction
to the right, marches four steps in the new direc-
tion, obliques to the right, places himself abreast
of the men on his right, and covers his file leader.
On the eleventh count, both ranks execute forward
march without command.

   d. Being in line, the commands to turn about
and halt are-SQUADS RIGHT (LEFT) ABOUT,
MARCH; SQUAD, HALT. SQUAD, HALT is given
after the completion of the first squad right. The
turn is executed as prescribed in b and c above,
except that all men, on arriving on the new line,
mark time until the command HALT is given.
HALT should be given on the tenth count.

            THE PLATOON

17. Organization

   Squads are numbered consecutively from right
to left in each platoon (fig. 13). These designa-
tions do not change. For convenience in giving
commands and for references, the designations
right, center, and left when in line, and leading,
center, and rear when in column are used to desig-
nate the actual right, center, and left, or leading,
center, and rear squads, respectively.

18. Movement of Guides and File Closers

   a. The guides of a column of squads place
themselves on the flank opposite to the file closers.
To change guides and file closers to the other
flank, the platoon leader commands-FILE CLOSERS
ON THE LEFT (RIGHT) FLANK, MARCH. At the
command MARCH, the file closers move through
the column to the other flank and the platoon
leader and guides change to the opposite flank.

   b. In executing movements where the new direc-
tion is toward the file closers, the platoon leader
commands-SQUADS RIGHT (LEFT) ABOUT, MARCH.
At the command of execution the file closers move
through the intervals between squads.

    THE COMPANY

22. Organization and Formations

   The drill company is comprised of two or more
platoons and company headquarters consisting of
the company commander, executive officer, first
sergeant, supply sergeant, and guidon bearer.
The sample formations in figures 16, 17, and 18
show three platoons of three squads each.

23. Rules for Close-Order Drill

   a. Platoon leaders repeat such preparatory
commands as are to be immediately executed by
their platoons, such as FORWARD and SQUADS
RIGHT, and the men execute the movement at the
command of execution, such as MARCH and
HALT, if applicable to their platoon. Thus, at the
company commander's command COLUMN OF
SQUADS, LEADING PLATOON, SQUADS RIGHT, the pla-
toon leader of the leading platoon repeats only the
command SQUADS RIGHT. In giving commands or
cautions, platoon leaders may prefix the numbers
of their platoons, as FIRST PLATOON or SECOND

PLATOON.

   b. The company executes commands like the
platoon.

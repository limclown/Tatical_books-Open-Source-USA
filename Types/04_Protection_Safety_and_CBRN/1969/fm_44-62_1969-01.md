41. Basic Principles

   a, Speed ring sights are the simplest but least

accurate of ADA AW fire control devices. They

are designed on the principle of similar triangles.

A small triangle formed by the rings and the

gunner's eye (peep sight) is similar to the slant
plane triangle in space, GT.T, (fig. 4-1). The
basic speed ring sight has a rear peep element
represented by the point @ and a serles of con-
centric circles as a front element, the center or

hub of which represents the point 7,’. The point
T,/ is represented at a point on one of the outer

concentric circles where the center of mass of the

target is carried while tracking. The concentric

circles are called speed rings, and the points
similar to the slant plane points G, 7,, and T, are

given the symbols G, T.’, and 7,

   b. The radius of the individual speed rings

depends on the range (D,) for which the sight

was designed, the distance the rings are placed

from the rear peep sight, and the target speeds

they represent,

    ¢. Most speed rings represent multiples of 100
miles per hour; one ring representing 100 miles
per hour, the next representing 200 miles per
hour, etc. For this reason, the English measuring

system (miles, yards, ete.) will be used in thie

chapter.

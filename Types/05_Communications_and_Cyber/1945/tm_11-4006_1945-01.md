     DESCRIPTION OF RADIO RECEIVER AND TRANSMITTER BC—474—A*

 |. General

Radio Receiver and Transmitter BC-474—A (figs. 1 and 2), a component of Radio Set SCR-288,
is a portable amplitude-modulated transmitter and receiver. It transmits and receives both voice-
modulated (phone) and c-w (continuous-wave) signals.

2. Transmitter
       a. USE. Radio Transmitter BC—474—A is used for ground operation.

        b. FREQUENCY RANGE. The 3-tube transmitter is designed to operate within the frequency
range of 3,500 to 6,300 ke (kilocycles).

       c. OPERATING RANGE. The operating range is 15 miles for c-w operation and 8 miles for
voice-modulated operation.

       d. POWER INPUT. Power is furnished by hand-operated Generator GN—44—A which sup-
plies 290 volts de (direct current) to the plate circuits and 6.6 volts dc to the heater circuits.

       e. POWER OUTPUT. The output of the transmitter is 4 watts for either phone or c-w opera-
tion. A 35-foot antenna and a 35-foot counterpoise are required for rated performance.

3. Receiver
       a. USE. Radio Receiver BC-474—A is used for the reception of phone or c-w transmissions.

Four tubes are employed in a superheterodyne circuit.
       b. FREQUENCY RANGE. The frequency range of the receiver is from 2,300 to 6,500 ke.
       c. POWER INPUT. Power is furnished by a dry cell power pack consisting of a 90-volt B

battery and a 1.5-volt A battery, or by hand-operated Generator GN—44—A, Filter FL—-10 and Cord
CD-125.

       d. POWER OUTPUT. Output circuits are provided for two sets of headphones. The receiver
is capable of delivering 100 milliwatts into a 4,000-ohm load.

4. Theory of Operation of Transmitter

      a. GENERAL. The block diagram of the transmitter (fig. 3) shows the path of the signal
from the m-o (master-oscillator) stage to the antenna and counterpoise.

       b. MASTER-OSCILLATOR STAGE. The m-o stage consists of a temperature-compensated elec-
tron-coupled oscillator which employs Tube JAN-6V6GT (VT-107—A). The frequency of oscilla-
tion is determined by a tuned circuit of the Hartley type. The ouput of the oscillator is developed
across a resistor in the plate circuit.

       c. POWER-AMPLIFIER STAGE. The output of the m-o stage is impressed on the grid of the
p-a (power-amplifier) Tube JAN-6V6GT (VT-107—A) through a coupling capacitor. The ampli-
fied output appears across the plate resonant circuit. Signal energy is coupled to the antenna
through an antenna-loading coil. For c-w operation, the cathode circuit of the power amplifier is
keyed. _

      d. MODULATOR STAGE. The microphone is inductively coupled by the input transformer to
the grid circuit of modulator Tube JAN-6V6GT (VT-107-A). The amplified audio-frequency
output of the modulator tube is applied to the plate and screen grid of the p-a tube through a

8. Disassembly for Cleaning and Inspecting

       a. REMOVAL OF CHASSIS. Loosen retainer trunk latches on sides of cabinet and lift up the
lid. Unplug and remove the headphone, key, microphone, battery plugs and filter from the cabinet
compartments. Withdraw battery cable from the battery socket. Detach stay-joints from the sides
of the cabinet by loosening thumb knobs and fold stay-joints into the lid. Open lid fully to permit
removal of chassis. Loosen the eight knurled-head panel screws holding the chassis to the cabinet,
until the screws clear their threaded seats. Do not withdraw screws completely. Slide the panel
forward and withdraw the chassis from the cabinet.

       b. REMOVAL OF TUBES. Remove the grid clips from the four receiver tubes and lift the
tubes out of their sockets with a slight rotary motion. Lift the three transmitter tubes out of their
sockets.

9. Cleaning, Inspecting, and Lubricating of Chassis

       a. CLEANING. Thorough cleaning of the radio set is necessary to insure optimum per-
formance and to prevent corrosion, rust, and dirt from damaging parts or causing arc-over or low-
resistance leakage between high-voltage parts and ground. Use clean, dry, low-pressure compressed
air to blow out all dust particles in the unit. Use a clean brush to remove dust from corners and
other spots difficult to reach. Exercise care when cleaning terminal contact points and switches,
jacks, binding posts, etc. Remove corrosion from terminals, pins and unpainted parts with a fine
grade of sandpaper. Do not use abrasives on switch contacts or nickel-plated surfaces. Apply dry-
cleaning solvent (SD) with a brush to switch contact points and other parts difficult to reach.
Blow out loosened particles with compressed air. Clean gears with a pipe cleaner dipped in dry-
cleaning solvent (SD).

       b. INSPECTION. Inspect wires for broken connections, frayed ends, shorts or poor insula-
tion. Check resistors, capacitors, chokes, and other elements for broken parts, charred spots and
other signs of overload. Inspect terminals to see that they are secure and properly soldered. Check
for chipped and broken parts in sockets, plugs, switches, jacks, binding posts and other parts.
Check switches for loose or bent contacts and terminal boards for broken lugs or signs of burning.
Tighten all screws and nuts. Straighten dial pointers, if bent, and test to see that they are secure.
Inspect tuning knobs to see that they do not rub against the panel and are securely fastened on the
shafts. Tighten the set screws. See that panel switches snap firmly into place at each contact
position. Straighten all bent parts of chassis. Repaint all painted surfaces that are worn or
chipped.

       c. LUBRICATING. Use lubricants sparingly. After cleaning gears with compressed air and
dry-cleaning solvent (SD) applied with a brush, lubricate with machine gear grease. Grease, —
Lubricating, Special (GL). Do not use oil. Lubricate switch contacts lightly with Oil, Lubricating,
Preservative, Special (PS), applying oil with a clean brush.

10. Cleaning, Inspecting, and Testing of Tubes

Remove shield from Tube JAN-1D8GT (V-4). Note position of grounding clip and be sure to place
it over the same pin when replacing shield. Clean pins of each tube with dry-cleaning solvent (SD)
applied with a brush. For heavier corrosion, use crocus. cloth applied with care on nickel-plated
surfaces of pins. Inspect tubes for bent pins, and for internal and external breaks. Shake tube to
detect loose pins and other loose elements. Check each tube on a tube tester, allowing sufficient time
for tube to warm up. Tap each tube gently during the test for loose and defective elements.

11. Replacement of Defective Parts

      a. Before replacing any part which, on inspection, indicates improper electrical operation,
or signs of overload, such as charring and burning, make a thorough electrical check of the circuit
to discover the cause of the improper operation. Check voltages and resistances, referring to the
tube socket voltages and resistances (fig. 5), and correct trouble before replacing defective parts.

      b. Replace all wires having charred or poor insulation. Resolder all poor or broken connec-
tions. Replace all defective tubes, resistors, capacitors, coils, transformers or other elements with
new ones. Replace all missing or defective screws, washers, nuts, and other parts.

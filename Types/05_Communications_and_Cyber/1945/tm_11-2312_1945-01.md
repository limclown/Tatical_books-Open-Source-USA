                          Section |. DESCRIPTION

 1. General (fig. 1)

 Camera Equipment PH-545/PF (V-Mail) is a semiautomatic, portable,
 document-copying set for recording documents on 35- or 16-mm perforated
 or unperforated photographic film. The unit operates on 115-volt alter-
 nating current or 115-volt direct current when used with a converter.
 The equipment consists of a camera (Recordak film unit model E), a
 tripod and easel assembly, reflector flood lamps, a light meter, a foot
 switch, and a power control unit. The automatic camera is electrically
 operated and photographs small documents placed horizontally on an easel
 attached to the tripod base, or larger documents mounted vertically on a
 wall support.

2. Application

 Camera Equipment PH-545/PF is designed for copying documents on
 film. It can be used to copy any type of document, map, or chart which
 can be accommodated by the 22%- by 33'%4-inch easel. When using
 35-mm unperforated film for horizontal operation, documents up to
 37% by 52% inches can be recorded. When 16-mm unperforated film is
 used for vertical operation, the camera can copy any document up to
 10% by 27 inches. Documents up to 17 by 45 inches can be copied when
 the unit is used horizontally. In Photomail stations, the equipment is used
 to supplement recorders which record V-Mail letters. Recorders cannot
 be used for maps, charts, official documents and other materials which
 differ in size from the standard V-Mail letter. Kodacrome film can be
 used in the camera to photograph documents in full color. The portability
 of the equipment makes it possible to move it easily from one location to
 another. This is particularly useful in the event that the secret nature of
 documents to be copied prohibits their transfer. The entire unit can be
 set up for operation in 10 or 15 minutes.

28. Meaning of Preventive Maintenance

Preventive maintenance is a systematic series of operations performed
at regular intervals on equipment, to eliminate major break-downs and
unwanted interruptions in service, and to keep the equipment operating
at top efficiency. To understand what is meant by preventive maintenance,
it is necessary to distinguish between preventive maintenance, trouble
shooting, and repair. The prime function of preventive maintenance is
to prevent break-downs and, therefore, the need for repair. On the
other hand, the prime function of trouble shooting and repair is to locate
and correct existing defects. The importance of preventive maintenance
cannot be overemphasized.

   Note. The operations in sections IV, V, and VI are first and second echelon
(organization operators and repairmen) maintenance.

   a. ELectricAL System (fig. 20). The electrical system of Camera

converter through two contacts. It leaves the converter as alternating

then returns to the relay to feed the drive motor and the transformers
of the equipment. Incorporated in the camera is an alarm system con-
sisting of an 8-volt buzzer and-a small transformer which supplies the
buzzer with current. The three alarm contacts are connected in parallel
so that actuation of any one of them will sound the buzzer.

39. Functioning of Parts

   a. APERTURE CONTROL AND Firm Apvance MECHANISM (figs. 22
and 23). The camera provided with Camera Equipment PH-—545/PF
has a controlling mechanism by which the amount of film advanced is
related to the area of film required to photograph a document. The film
advance control mechanism is correlated with the aperture adjustment
so that adjusting the aperture to accommodate a document automatically
stops the film advance when enough film has been moved forward to
photograph that document. This is accomplished in the manner described
below.

   (1) The film drive motor is coupled by a connecting rod to a quadrant
gear. The teeth of the quadrant gear engage a film advance gear which
supports and actuates a film advance pawl. This pawl engages teeth on
the perimeter of a film drive ratchet wheel which is fastened to the film
drive roller shaft.

   (2) A roller on the side of the film advance pawl rides on a film
advance cam. This cam controls the amount of film advanced, by lifting
the pawl away from the teeth of the ratchet wheel, thus disconnecting the
film drive.

   (3) The position of the film advance cam is shifted by operating the
aperture adjusting knob on the outside of the camera. The mechanism
is designed so that when, for example, the aperture is set at 1 inch, the
film advance pawl will be lifted off the ratchet wheel when 1 inch of film,
plus a slight surplus for image separation, has been advanced.

   (4) The aperture plates are connected to two racks which engage the
opposite sides of a pinion. When the pinion is turned, the racks move
in opposite directions. Turning the aperture control knob on the outside
of the camera drives the pinion. This pinion drives the racks which move

the aperture plates either away from, or toward each other to increase or

decrease the aperture width.

              Section VII]. TROUBLE SHOOTING

40. Trouble Shooting Technique

 Troubles are rarely encountered with Camera Equipment PH-545/PF
 unless the equipment has been subjected to rough handling or unsual
 conditions during operation. Eventually, however, normal wear will cause
 trouble and necessitate the replacement of worn parts. The following
 subparagraphs will serve as a guide and offer a procedure for locating
 trouble.

     a. ELectricAL Trousies (fig. 20). (1) General. Continuity test
 equipment such as a battery with bell, buzzer, light, or voltmeter, will
 be found useful in tracing electrical circuits. Reference to the wiring
 diagram (fig. 20) should be made whenever electrical troubles are
 encountered.

     (2) Connecting cords. .Whenever electrical failure occurs in the
 entire equipment, the electrical cords should be inspected for breaks, bad
  connections, or short circuits. When electrical trouble occurs in the
  camera, the interconnecting cord should be tested, and the defective
 circuit traced from the power control unit to the questionable parts of the
 camera. If the trouble lies in a defective connection or a broken con-
  necting wire, repairs may be made by soldering the connection or replacing
  the wire. If the trouble lies in a defective part such as a transformer,
  relay, motor, etc., the part must be replaced.

     b. OpticAL Trousies (fig. 21). The optical system performs two

  functions. It transmits light from the document to the film, and transmits

  light from the projection lamp to the easel. Troubles that occur in the
  finder system are confined to the projection lamp, the condensers, the
  reflecting mirror, the etched glass, the mirror shutter, and the camera
  lens. Troubles that occur in the exposure system are confined to the
  camera, projection lens, or the mirror shutter. No difficulty should be
  encountered in isolating optical troubles. Correction usually will require
_ replacement of the defective parts.

   Caution: Critical setting of the camera lens is absolutely essential for
proper operation. Under no condition should the camera lens be removed
for repairs in the field.

   c. MECHANICAL TROUBLES. Mechanical troubles may be remedied by
either repairing or replacing the defective part. Troubles occuring in the
iripod or easel will be obvious to the maintenance man, and should be
corrected easily. The correction of mechanical troubles which may occur
inside the camera, particularly within the mechanism compartment,
requires a thorough understanding of the.theory of operation as covered
in paragraphs 37 and 38. By opening the camera compartments and
loading the camera with leader, the maintenance man can observe the
camera in operation. If the functions of the various parts are understood,
it is not difficult for the maintenance man to isolate and replace the
defective parts, as described in paragraphs 43 and 44.

42. Adjusting Film Advance Mechanism

To adjust the film advance mechanism for proper spacing of domuments,
proceed as directed below.

   a. Note the average space between documents on a length of film cov-
ering several images.

   b. Note the position of the film-advance cam with respect to a pencil
mark placed on the perimeter of the film drive rachet wheel.

   c. Loosen the two screws holding the film advance cam. The screws
are accessible through holes in the film drive rachet wheel.

   d. Move the film advance cam in a counterclockwise direction to in-
crease the space between images and in a clockwise direction to decrease
the space. The cam should be moved a distance on the perimeter of the
film drive rachet wheel equal to approximately 114 times the increase or
decrease required. To increase the space between documents 1% inch
for example, mark the cam position, loosen the cam screws, and shift the
cam 3/16 inch in a counterclockwise direction.

46. Minimum Test Requirements

Minimum test requirements on Camera Equipment PH-545/PF will de-
pend upon the repair work accomplished, or the parts that were replaced.
If, for example, a projection lamp must be replaced, it is necessary to test
only the lamp and the finder system. If a major repair operation such as
replacing the motor cam assembly, must be performed, the equipment
should be set up and operated, and the resultant negatives checked for
quality, space between the images, etc. The operator should see paragraph
25 when checking the equipment after repairs, adjustments, or replace-
ments have been made.

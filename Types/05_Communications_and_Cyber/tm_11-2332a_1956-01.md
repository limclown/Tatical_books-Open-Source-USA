3. Purpose and Use
    Still Picture Projector AP-6(1) (fig. 1) projects still pictures

from 35-millimeter (mm) perforated film strips or from standard
35-mm slides in 2 by 2-inch mounts. The equipment is intended
for use in large classrooms or theaters as a visual aid in teaching
or briefing.

5. Descrintion

    a. Projector. The projector (fig. 2), includes eight aluminum
castings that support the projector mechanical parts and optics:
the base, housing, housing cover, nosepiece, motor mount, turret
assembly, fan shroud, and elevating leg assembly.

    b. Carrying Case. The plywood carrying case is equipped with
two carrying handles, one on each end (fig. 1). The spare projec-
tion lamp is stored in the top section of the carrying case and the
slide carrier is stored in a compartment in the bottom section
(fig. 3).

   ce. Film Strip Mechanism. ‘the film strip mechanism (fig. 6)
fits into the turret assembly. The single frame screw knob, the
film advance knob, and the framing clutch handle are located
right side of the film strip mechanism. The film sprocket assembly
is mounted in the bottom and the glass aperture assemblies and
aperture adjustment masks are mounted on the front of the film
strip mechanism.

   d. Shde Carrier. The chrome plated slide carrier (fig. 7) fits
into the turret assembly. The slide carrier pusher is located on
the left and the stop lever knob is located on the front of the slide
carrier.

14. Preloading and Cleaning

   a. Projector.   When in use, clean the projector daily before it
is operated.

(1) Dust the outer surface of the projection lens assembly
      with a camel’s-hair brush.

(2) Dampen a lens tissue with liquid lens cleaner and wipe
      off any dust that cannot be removed with the camel’s-
      hair brush. Use a circular motion to wipe the lens.

(8) Dry the lens with a clean dry lens tissue.

   Caution: Do not use any cleaning fluid that has not been
approved for use on lens. Do not touch the lens with the

fingers.

   b. Film Strip Mechanism.  Clean the film strip mechanism (fig.
6) as follows:

(1) Remove dust from the film strip mechanism with a soft
      camel’s-hair brush. Avoid scratching the glass pressure

          plates.

(2) Remove all emulsion or dirt from the film strip mechan-
      ism with an orange stick or toothpick dipped in Cleaning
      Compound Federal stock No. 7980—895-9545 (par. 31).

             Warning: Provide adequate ventilation when using
          cleaning compound. Prolonged breathing of the fumes is
          dangerous. Cleaning compound is inflammable; never
          use it near a flame.

(3) Remove dirt, dust, or lint from the edge of the aperture
      opening.

    Section Ill. OPERATION UNDER UNUSUAL CONDITIONS

20. General

    The projector is intended for operation under shelter at mild
temperatures, where unusual conditions are not encountered.
However, should the equipment be used under unusual climatic
conditions, special operating precautions must be taken.

21. Operation in Arctic Climates

    When the equipment has been stored outdoors or in an unheated
shelter, or has been kept at low temperatures for an extended
period of time, follow the procedure explained below before oper-
ating the equipment.

qa. Gradually transfer the equipment from the cold to the

warmer temperature. Allow 6 hours or more for the equipment
to reach room temperature.

    b. Whenever possible, while the equipment is in cold atmos-
phere, inclose the unpacked equipment in water repellent material,
such as waterproof bags, a shelter cloth, or other improvised cov-
ering. The water repellent material must be as airtight as possi-
ble. After the equipment has been transferred to the warmer
room, keep the covering on for approximately 6 hours to permit
the equipment to reach the room temperature. ‘This procedure
will further reduce the possibility of condensation on the equip-
ment.

    c. Before using the equipment, use a lint-free cloth to remove
any water that has condensed on the outside surfaces of the equip-
ment, the glass pressure plates, and the film riding surfaces. Clean
the condensing lenses with lens tissue. When moisture has con-
densed on the inside surface of the projection lens assembly, keep
the lens at room temperature until it is clear. To dry the lens
rapidly, move the FAN and LAMP switches to the on position

22. Operation in Desert Climates

    When the equipment has been used outdoors where dust, wind,
and sand are present, these abrasive materials must be removed
to avoid damage to the projector.

    a. Use a camel’s-hair brush to clean the pressure plates, all
film riding surfaces, and the projection lens assembly. Do not
use lens tissue to clean the lens unless it first has been dusted with
a camel’s-hair brush. Cleaning the lens with tissue before it has
been dusted will scratch the lens. Do this cleaning indoors or
under cover.

    b. Avoid storing the projector outdoors if possible. However,
when this is necessary, cover it with a sheltering cloth or other
improvised material to protect it from dust.

23. Operation in Tropical Climates

  a. If it is necessary to store the projector outdoors in tropical
areas, cover the equipment with a sheltering cloth to protect it
from moisture.

32. Projection Lamp Replacement

   a. Unscrew the three captive screws in the housing cover and
lift the housing cover from the housing.

   b. Press down on the top of the projection lamp (fig. 12) and
turn it 14, turn to the left (counterclockwise). Remove the lamp.

   Warning: Do not touch the projection lamp with bare fingers

   c. Position the new projection lamp so that the small flange is

jector turret in accordance with the picture format.

38. Film Strip Mechanism

   The film strip mechanism is used to project a series of pictures
on one strip. It transports the film strip from frame to frame,
either single or double, and indexes each frame SO that it is always
positioned properly on the screen.

   a. For double frame projection, the glass aperture assembly is
placed behind the center plate, and for single frame projection, the
glass aperture assembly is placed in front of the center plate. Asa
result, the film strip in the glass aperture assembly is in one of
the positions shown in figure 15. This procedure allows the maxi-
mum uniformly distributed light to pass through the entire ier
or slide area for both types of projection.

   b. For double frame projection, the film advance knob is ro-
tated 14 turn (14 revolution), and for single frame projection, 14,

turn. The amount of rotation is controlled by the single frame
screw knob. The face of the clutch bearing (20, fig. 24) has a slot
milled across it. For double frame projection, the single frame

screw knob is rotated clockwise as far as possible, withdrawing
the detent rod into the film advance knob. When the detent has
been withdrawn, only the spring loaded ball bearing will engage
the slot in the face of the clutch bearing and stop the rotation
after each 14 turn. For single frame projection, the single frame
screw knob is turned clockwise as far as possible moving the de-

clutch. The spring-loaded ball bearing and the detent are at a
90° angle to each other. As a result, either the ball bearing or the
detent will engage the slot in the face of the clutch bearing and
stop the rotation after each 14 turn of the film advance knob.

39. Slide Carrier

    The slide carrier is used to carry the mounted slides into align-
ment with the projector aperture. It has two compartments for
slides so tha: while one slide is being projected, the preceding slide
can be removed and the next slide inserted, ready for projection.

40. Inspecting

   Inspection by sight and touch often will determine the general
condition of the projector and the amount of repair required. In-
spect the equipment as follows:

   a. Perform the visual inspection outlined in SOG e At 31.
   b. Inspect the projection lens assembly for chips, scratches,
cracks, or breaks. If the projection lens assembly is damaged,
replace it (par. 41D).
   c. Inspect the front and rear condensing lenses for chips,
scratches, cracks, or breaks, If a condensing lens is damaged,
replace it (par. 41c and d).
   d. Inspect the heat filter for cracks or breaks. Scratches on the
filter do not make the filter useless. Replace a cracked, split, or
broken filter immediately to prevent damage to the film (par. 41¢
and d).

41. Stripping

   a. Stripping consists of removing the projection lens assembly,
the projection lamp, the rear condensing lens, the heat filter, and
the front condensing lens.

   b. Remove the projection lens assembly (1, fig. 19) by pulling
the assembly out of the nosepiece (6). The projection lens assem-
bly is held in place by a detent pin (5).

   c. Loosen the three cover locking screws (85, fig. 17) and re-
move the housing cover (84). The rear condensing lens (39), the
heat filter (40), and the front condensing lens (41) now can be
reached for removal. They are supported and aligned by four lens
support spacer studs (45) and the funnel assembly (42).

           Section IV. ALINEMENT OF OPTICAL SYSTEM

54. General
   a. The projection lens, front and rear condensing lenses, heat

filter, projection lamp, and reflector, have a definite relationship
to each other, and their alignment is very important. All the com-
ponents are fixed; however, the reflector mounting assembly and
the projection lamp socket can be adjusted if necessary.

   b. Under ordinary usage it never should be necessary to aline
the optics. The instructions in paragraph 55 apply only when
considerable abuse and possible shifting of parts have occurred.
Major adjustments often indicate a damaged instrument; there-
fore, prior to a complete optics alinement, it is advisable to check
the projector (par. 44) to determine whether it should be recon-
ditioned or returned for replacement.

   ce. Uneven or spotty illumination indicates misalinement. If
this occurs, try-another lamp before realigning the optical system.

   d. Maximum lumen output and uniformity of illumination is
obtained by having the lamp filaments positioned evenly in the
aperture when the filaments are viewed through the lens and the
reflector is positioned for optimum output and uniformity of
screen corner illumination.

56. General
    This section is used as a guide to determine the quality of a

repaired projector. The final test outlined in paragraph 57 may
be performed by maintenance personnel with adequate test equip-

57. Test Equipment Required for Final Testing

   Test equipment repaired for final testing the quality of a re-
paired or. overhauled projector is Foot Candle Photoelectric Meter
ME-86/U. This meter is used to check the intensity and uni-
formity of the light projected on the screen after the equipment
has been repaired.

58. Final Testing Procedure

   Make final tests with the projector Sparithite under actual op-
erating conditions.

   a. Set up the projector and plug the power cable assembly into
a 117-volt, 50- to 60-cps as power source.

  -b. Inspect the elevating leg assembly to see that it is in proper
working order. The elevating leg assembly should elevate the-
projector to an angle of 10° with respect to the horizontal.

   c. Move the FAN and LAMP switches to the on position. Posi-
tion the projector to fill an area of 40 by 28 inches (approximate-
ly 12.5 feet from the screen). The illuminated area must be free
from masks, color rings or bands, dirt marks, and foreign matter.
Dark lines indicate that one or more of the optical elements are
cracked. Spots and smudges indicate dirt in the optics.

   d. Check the intensity of illumination at the center of the screen
and the uniformity of illumination at the corners. Illumination at
the center must be 125 foot-candles +5 percent. Uniformity of
iluminations must be as follows. If the projector does not meet
these requirements, refer to paragraph 55 and realine the pro-
jector.

        (1) Average of the four corners must be not less than 75
              percent of illumination reading obtained at the center of
              the screen.

        (2) Lowest corner reading must be not less than 65 percent.
    e. Project an image onto the screen and focus the projector.
The projected image must be clear, sharp, and evenly illuminated.
   f. Use both the film strip mechanism and the slide carrier to be
sure that both are in proper condition.

59. Disassembly

   Use the following instructions when preparing the projector for
transportation and storage:

   a. Loosen the elevating leg locking knob. Return the elevating
leg assembly to minimum height so that the legs are recessed in
the base of projector; tighten the elevating leg locking knob.

   b. If slides were the last thing to be projected, loosen the acces-
sory locking knob and remove the slide carrier from the projector
turret. Reinsert the film strip mechanism. Tighten the accessory
locking knob and return the slide carrier to the bottom of the
carrying case; secure it in place with the provided straps.

   ce. Disconnect the power cable assembly and wrap it around the
projector as shown in figure 1.

   d. Set the projector in the bottom section of the carrying case.

   e. Check the contents of the projector set against the table of
components (par. 4).

   f. Cover the projector with the top section of the carrying case
and lock the four latch fasteners.

60. Repacking for Shipment or Limited Storage

   Figure 4 illustrates one method of repacking the equipment.
Specific directions for packing the projector are as follows:

   a. Place the projector and technical manuals within a close-
fitting, slotted, corrugated fiberboard box. Seal the entire closure
with gummed tape and blunt all corners of the box.

   b. Place carboard corners for spacing around each corner of
the fiberboard box.

   c. Place the fiberboard box and corners spacers into another
close-fitting, slotted, corrugated fiberboard box. Seal the entire
closure with gummed tape and blunt all corners of the box.

61. Methods of Destruction

   Note. The demolition procedures outlined in a through e below will be
used to prevent the enemy from using or salvaging this equipment. Demoli-
tion of the equipment will be accomplished only upon order of the commander.

   a. Smash. Smash the projector housing, base, elevating leg as-
sembly, turret, projection lens barrel, rear condensing lens, heat
filter, front condensing lens, reflector, lamps, film strip mecha-
nism, slide carrier, and carrying case. Use sledges, axes, handaxes,
pickaxes, hammers, crowbars, or other heavy tools to destroy
this equipment.

   b. Cut. Cut the power cables and films; use axes, handaxes, or
machetes.

   c. Burn. Burn the films and instruction books; use gasoline,
kerosene, oil, flame throwers, or incendiary grenades.

  d. Bend. Bend the cardboard slide mounts.

   e. Dispose. Bury or scatter the destroyed parts in slit trenches,
foxholes, or throw them into streams.

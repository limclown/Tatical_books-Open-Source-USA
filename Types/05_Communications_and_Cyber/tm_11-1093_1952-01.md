RESCUE.

   In case of electric shock, shut off the high voltage at once and ground
the circuits. If the high voltage cannot be turned off without delay, free
the victim from contact with the live conductor as promptly as possible.
Avoid direct contact with either the live conductor or the victim’s body.
Use a dry board, dry clothing, or other nonconductor to free the victim.
An ax may be used to cut the high-voltage wire. Use extreme caution to
avoid the resulting electric flash.

SYMPTOMS.

   a. Breathing stops abruptly in electric shock if the current passes
through the breathing center at the base of the brain. If the shock has
not been too severe, the breath center recovers after a while and normal
breathing is resumed, provided that a sufficient supply of air has been
furnished meanwhile by artificial respiration.

   b. The victim is usually very white or blue. The pulse is very weak or
entirely absent and unconsciousness is complete. Burns are usually pres-
ent. The victim’s body may become rigid or stiff in a very few minutes.
This condition is due to the action of electricity and is not to be consid-
ered rigor mortis. Artificial respiration must still be given, as several
such cases are reported to have recovered. The ordinary and general
tests for death should never be accepted.

TREATMENT.

   a. Start artificial respiration immediately. At the same time send for
a medical officer, if assistance is available. Do not leave the victim un-
attended. Perform artificial respiration at the scene of the accident,
unless the victim’s or operator’s life is endangered from such action. Jn
this case only, remove the victim to another location, but no farther than
is necessary for safety. If the new location is more than a few feet away,
artificial respiration should be given while the victim is being moved.
If the method of transportation prohibits the use of the Shaeffer prone
pressure method, other methods of resuscitation may be used. Pressure
may be exerted on the front of the victim’s diaphragm, or the direct
mouth-to-mouth method may be used. Artificial respiration, once started,
must be continued, without loss of rhythm.

   b. Lay the victim in a prone position, one arm extended directly over-
head, and the other arm bent at the elbow so that the back of the hand
supports the head. The face should be turned away from the bent elbow
so that the nose and mouth are free for breathing.

   c. Open the victim’s mouth and remove any foreign bodies, such as
false teeth, chewing gum, or tobacco. The mouth should remain open,

RELIEVING OPERATOR.

     The relief operator kneels beside the operator and follows him through
  several complete cycles. When the relief operator is sure he has the
 correct rhythm, he places his hands on the operator’s hands without
  applying pressure. This indicates that he is ready to take over. On the
  backward swing, the operator moves and the relief operator takes his
  position. The relieved operator follows through several complete cycles
  to be sure that the new operator has the correct rhythm. He remains
  alert to take over instantly if the new operator falters or hesitates on
  the cycle.

STIMULANTS.

   a. If an inhalant stimulant is used, such as aromatic spirits of am-
monia, the individual administering the stimulant should first test it
himself to see how close he can hold the inhalant to his own nostril for
comfortable breathing. Be sure that the inhalant is not held any closer
to the victim’s nostrils, and then for only 1 or 2 seconds every minute.

   b. After the victim has regained consciousness, he may be given hot
coffee, hot tea, or a glass of water containing 12 teaspoon of aromatic
spirits of ammonia. Do not give any liquids to an unconscious victim.

CAUTIONS.

   a. After the victim revives, keep him LYING QUIETLY. Any injury a
person may have received may cause a condition of shock. Shock is
present if the victim is pale and has a cold sweat, his pulse is weak and
rapid, and his breathing is short and gasping.

   b. Keep the victim lying flat on his back, with his head lower than the
rest of his body and his hips elevated. Be sure that there is no tight
clothing to restrict the free circulation of blood or hinder natural breath-
ing. Keep him warm and quiet.

   c. A resuscitated victim must be watched carefully as he    may    sud-
denly stop breathing. Never leave a resuscitated person alone  until  it is
CERTAIN that he is fully conscious and breathing normally.

3. Purpose and Use

   Dummy Antenna TS—235/UP provides a power absorbing ter-
mination for the r-f (radio-frequency) transmission line of a
radar set operating in the frequency range of 500 to 1,500 mc
(megacycles). The dummy antenna allows the radar transmitter
to be operated without radiating energy into space. The input end
of the dummy antenna contains a probe and a jack that are used
to connect a wavemeter or an echo box to the r-f line for test pur-
poses.

7. Description

   Dummy Antenna TS-—235/UP (fig. 1) consists of a tapered co-
axial structure containing a high-loss dielectric, an input coupling,
and a cooling fan. The input coupling is fitted with a probe and
jack for use in connecting test equipment to the dummy antenna.
The r-f input energy is converted into heat which is dissipated
by a large number of cooling fins surrounding the coaxial struc-
ture. The fins are surrounded by a housing in which the fan is
mounted. A cable for connecting power to the fan motor is per-
manently fastened to the motor at one end and is equipped with
an AN-3106 14S-—2P connector at the other end. The input end
of the dummy antenna is threaded for coupling to the radar trans-
mission line. An adapter is provided for connecting the center
conductor of the dummy antenna to the center conductor of the
radar transmission line. The adapter is stored in a receptacle in
one end of the dummy antenna housing. No spare parts are sup-
plied with the dummy antenna.

Section |. PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE SERVICES

 12. General Preventive Maintenance Techniques

       Preventive maintenance is work performed on equipment
   (usually when the equipment is not in use) to keep it in good
   working order so that breakdowns and needless interruptions in
   service will be kept to a minimum. Preventive maintenance dif-
   fers from trouble shooting and repair since its object is to prevent
   certain troubles from occurring. See AR 750-5.

      a. Use No. 000 sandpaper to remove corrosion.
      b. Use a clean, dry, lint-free cloth or a dry brush for cleaning.
      c. If necessary, except for electrical contacts, moisten the cloth
   or brush with Solvent, dry-cleaning (SD) ; then wipe the parts dry
  with a cloth.
      d. For further information on preventive maintenance tech-
- niques, refer to TB SIG 178.

13. Tools and Materials

   Tools and materials required for performing   preventive  mainte-

15. Lubrication

   The fan motor of Dummy Antenna TS—235/UP is equipped with
sealed bearings to prevent entrance of dirt and escape of lubricant.
Sealed bearings are packed during manufacture with the correct
amount and grade of grease to last the life of the bearing at nor-
mal operating temperatures. If the lubricant runs out of the bear-
ing, the bearing must be replaced with a new one. No attempt
should be made to apply oil or solvent to the bearings for the pur-
pose of supplementing or otherwise modifying the special grease
originally placed in the bearings.

    CHAPTER 4
      THEORY

20. Theory of Coaxial-line Load

   The coaxial-line load section of the dummy antenna consists of
a 134-inch diameter brass tube, a center conductor, and a resistive
material molded into the brass tube (outer conductor) (fig. 5).
The resistive material is a mixture of graphite and a special ce-
ment that is formed into a conical shape within the outer tube.
The inner conductor is held in place at the input end by an insula-
tor that is cemented in place. The other end of the center conduc-
tor is short-circuited to the outer tube by an end plate. The tapered
portion of the resistive element: provides a smooth impedance
transformation from the 50-ohm impedance of the dummy an-
tenna to the impedance of the transmission line. R-f power flow-
ing into the dummy antenna is gradually absorbed in the resistive
element as the power travels toward the end of the center conduc-
tor. The r-f power is thereby converted into heat which is con-
ducted through the outer conductor walls into the cooling fins.
The cooling fins present a large metal-to-air surface. Air is forced
through the fins by a fan. With the fan in operation, it is possible
to dissipate 1,000 watts of average r-f power within the dummy
antenna. The length of the coaxial-line load section is determined,
experimentally, to provide the lowest voltage standing wave ratio,
the proper characteristic impedance, and for optimum perform-
ance over a broad band of frequencies (500 to 1,500 mc).

21. Theory. of Test Probe

which projects at right angles to the coaxial-line load, and a center
conductor that extends into the input cavity of the dummy antenna
(fig. 5). The center conductor extends into the electric field and
acts aS a small antenna to extract a small portion of the power
flowing into the dummy antenna. The amount of power extracted
is determined by the depth of the probe in the electric field. The
probe depth, and therefore the ratio of input power to power ex-
tracted by the probe, is adjusted during manufacture. The fixed
power ratio is of little value when making tests through the test

24. Recalibration of Test Probe

   Recalibration of the attenuation of the test probe can be accom-

that is known to be calibrated correctly. Proceed as follows:

   a. Connect the dummy antenna that is known to be correct to
a radar set operating within the frequency band of 500 to 1,500
mc.

   b. Connect an echo box to the test probe of the dummy antenna,

using the cord furnished with the echo box.

   c. Place the radar set in operation and set the echo box for maxi-
mum ringtime as shown on the radar scope.

   d. Turn off the radar set but do not disturb the setting of the
echo box or the tuning of the radar equipment.

   é. Remove the dummy antenna from the radar r-f line and in-
stall the dummy antenna which is to be recalibrated. Connect the
echo box in the same manner as in Db above.

   f. Place the radar set in operation.

   g. Loosen the three screws in the test probe and adjust the
depth of the probe until the ringtime as shown on the radar scope
is exactly the same as obtained in SUPE ARee raec above. Then
fasten the probe in place by tightening the screws.

   h. Turn. off the radar set and disconnect the dummy antenna.

                                 DESCRIPTION

1. PURPOSE.

        a. The 13A (SPL) Transmission Measuring Set is designed for use in
making transmission measurements where portable apparatus is required to
measure received testing power.

        b. The 183A (SPL) Transmission Measuring Set has component parts spe-
cially treated with moisture-resistant materials for use under tropical con-

ditions and differs in this respect from the 13A Transmission Measuring Set.
The circuit and operating features, however, are the same for both the 13A

and 13A (SPL) sets. The 134 (SPL) Transmission Measuring Set is manufactured
as the D-165655 set.

&. RANGE.

         This set has an input impedance of approximately 600 ohms and is capa-
ble of measuring received power from -45 dbm (45 db below a milliwatt into
600 ohms) to +10 dbm (10 db above a milliwatt into 600 ohms) over a fre-
quency range from 30 cycles to 15 kilocycles. Anexternal souor f sc tane dard
testing power of 1 MW (one milliwatt) is required for gain or loss measure-
ments.

3. SIZE AND WEIGHT.

        The 13A (SPL) Transmission Measuring Set is provided in portable form

equipped with a power-supply cord and removable cover. It is approximately

11 inches long, 6-1/2 inches wide, and 8-1/2 inches high, weighing about

14 pounds.

     6. METER SCALES.

         Measurements are indicated on a 5-db range meter scale which is marked
in 0.l-db divisions. Adial switch operates a potentiometer circuitin steps
of 5 db each with step designations which indicate the amountto be added to
the meter scale. This provides an adjustment of the input of the amplifier-
rectifier circuit so that any received power in the range of the set can be
measured in reference to one milliwatt. With an input of one milliwatt and
the dial switch at either zero position, the meter scale deflection will be
zero on the meter scale corresponding to the dial switch setting. The dial
steps designated in red are associated with the red or upper scale of the
meter and indicate received power greater than one milliwatt. On the other
steps, designated in white, the received power is less than one milliwatt
and the black or lower meter scale is used.

8. GENERAL.

        The 13A (SPL) Transmission Measuring Set should alwaysbe operated with
the panel in a horizontal position in orderto insure proper heat dissipation
from the vacuum tubes and resistors and to assure correct readings on the
meter.

9. INPUT LIMITS.

        a. Do not connect the input of this set to any circuit having  an out-
put in excess of 10 db above one milliwatt. Where output levels        of more

than 10 db above a milliwatt are to be read an attenuator, suchas the  5A (SPL)
should be inserted between the output of the circuit under test         and the
input of the 138A (SPL) Transmission Measuring Set.

        b. This set should not be connected to a circuit which would cause

direct current to flow in the primary winding of the input transformer. If
circuits having a d-c component in the transmission path are to be measured
a 2-microfarad capacitor should be connected in series with each of the
leads to the INPUT binding posts of the 13A (SPL) Transmission Measuring Set.
Insertion of these capacitors will introduce a transmission loss which will
vary with frequency. To correct for this transmission loss, first connect
the oscillator to the 138A (SPL) Transmission Measuring Set through the series
capacitors, and increase the oscillator output until the 13A (SPL) Trans-
mission Measuring Set reads 0 db. Then read the oscillator output. The
oscillator output reading will be greater than one milliwatt by the loss of
the capacitors. Use this vaol f ou scile lator output insteadof one milliwatt
in making measurements. The 13A (SPL) Transmission Measuring Set will then
read the loss or gain of the equipment directly as the loss of the capac-
itors will be corrected for by the increase in oscillator output. The os-
cillator output should be readjusted for each testing frequency because the
capacitor loss varies with frequency.

        17. GENERAL.

         a. The 13A (SPL) Transmission Measuring Set has been designed for
measuring circuits of 600-ohm impedance in terms of the standard testing
power of one milliwatt. The results are indicated in db above or below this
power. When the impedance of the circuit is other than 600 ohms, it may.
be necessary to make corrections as discussed in paragraph 16. When making
amplifier gain measurements, it will be necessary to provide loss pads in
connection with the sending power and to make sending power corrections as
explained in paragraph 15.

         b. The performance informatasigoivnen, assutmhae t s good vacuum tubes
are employed and that the power supply to the set has been turned on for
about 5 minutes to allow for proper operation of the heater-type tubes. It
also assumes that the power-supply voltage is within the range from 105 to
125 volts and that the set has been calibrated using precisely one milliwatt
at 1000 cycles.

18. TYPE OF CONSTRUCTION.

         This apparatus is of a panel-chassis type of construction and is con-
tained in a perforated steel housing. The box is provided with a removable
cover to protect the surface apparatus. The perforations in the housing
assist in dissipating the heat generated, and the set should be exposed to
freely circulating air while in operation. This is an important considera-
tion in the use of the set due to its compactness and the effects of heat.
on the life of certain parts, particularly the electrolytic capacitors. The
effects of temperature variations on the electrical performance have been
Minimized by the circuit design.

26. GENERAL.

         The periodic maintenance of the 13A (SPL) Transmission Measuring Set
may be confined principally to a check of the vacuum tubes. Failure of the
set to operate properly will necessitate removing the chassis from the box
(see Safety Notice, pageVII) and examining the equipment for a defective
vacuum tube, electrolytic capacitor, or a broken wire.

28. METER.

         This is a self-contained, moving coil, permanent  magnet,  suppressed
zero-type meter housed in a flush-type bakelite case.

29. DEFECTIVE CAPACITORS.

         The electrolytic capacitors should be replaced when found defective.
This usually will be evidenced by instabilityof calibration. Unsatisfactory
performance not due to vacuum tubes will probably be an indication of de-
fective capacitors.

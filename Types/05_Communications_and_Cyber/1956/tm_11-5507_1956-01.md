                   Section Il. DESCRIPTION AND DATA

3. Description

           (figs. 1-5)

   The rotary converters, covered in this manual, are fan-venti-
lated, semi-inclosed units. Filter boxes are located either on the
top or the side of all units, except PU-141A/U. Inspection covers
are provided on both ends and mounting feet are provided to sup-
port the rotary converter on the floor or foundation.

4. Purpose and Use

   The rotary converters, covered in this manual, are used to con-
vert various de voltages to 110-volt, 60-cycle per second (cps), ac,
at a power factor of not lower than 80 percent. They are intended
primarily to supply ac power for fixed station and shipborne radio
receiving and transmitting equipment.

    INSTALLATION AND OPERATION

7. Unpacking and Checking

   Unpack the rotary converter at the location where it will be in-
stalled. The location should be convenient to the load equipment
and to the de power source.

    a. Withdraw the nails that secure the top of the wooden box,

with a nail puller. Be careful not to damage the equipment when

removing the packing material.        |

   b. Lift off the top of the box and remove the technical litera-
ture.

   c. Slit the barrier material along the seal and open the barrier.
Slit the inner paper seal and remove the paper blocking.

   d. Lift the rotary converter from  the fiberboard  box. Be sure
that the fiberboard-wrapped spare     parts are not   thrown away
with the packaging material.

   e. Inspect the unpackaged equipment for any evidence of dam-
age through rough handling or improper shipment.

   f. If any damage is noted, forward DD Form 6 in accordance
with instructions in paragraph 2).

8. Installation

   a. Provide a foundation that will keep the rotary converter
about 6 inches above the floor or deck. Allow about 2 feet of
space on all sides of the rotary converter to enable servicing and
to permit a free circulation of air.

    b. Fasten the rotary converter to the foundation with bolts or
lag screws. If sensitive electronic equipment is installed close by,
install vibration dampening material between the foundation and
the base of the rotary converter.

9. Connections

141A/U, are provided with a female output receptacle for the ac
output connection. The de input is connected to the input leads
that are provided on all models of the equipments.

   b. Input Connections. Connect the de power source to a fused
safety switch and connect the input leads of the rotary converter

11. Scope of Responsibility |

   a. First Echelon. First echelon personnel should keep the ex-
terior of the equipment clean, inspect the general condition of the
equipment, and see that it has not been damaged.

   b. Second Echelon. Second echelon personnel should tighten any
loose screws, nuts and bolts, replace worn brushes, and clean the
commutator and slip rings. Second echelon personnel should test
and adjust output voltage and frequency and should make such
minor repairs as available tools and spare parts permit.

14. Organizational Maintenance Operations

   a. Operator’s Maintenance. Each day that the equipment is
used, inspect it thoroughly to see that it is not damaged. Wipe the
exterior with a clean cloth to remove all dust and dirt. Use clean-
ing solvent (SD) if necessary. While the equipment is operating,
be alert for unusual noise, vibration, signs of overheating, and
other indications of faulty operation.

    b. Second Echelon Maintenance.

        (1) Weekly. Once each week, inspect all exposed wiring for
              cuts, cracked insulation, loose terminals and connections,
               and other unsatisfactory conditions. Inspect all fasten-
               ings and tighten those in need of tightening. Remove
             the inspection covers and inspect the commutator, slip
               rings, and brushes. Operate the equipment and note if
              there is excessive sparking at the brushes. Perform any
               lubrication that may be scheduled.

       (2) Monthly. Once each month, remove the inspection covers
              and inspect the brushes, brush holders, and brush
              springs. Remove the brushes from their holders and
               measure their length. Replace any brush that is worn
              to less than three-eighths-inch or that is cracked, chipped,
               or otherwise unsatisfactory. Make sure that the brush

holders are securely mounted and clean. See that the
brushes move freely in their holders and that they make

15. Care of Commutator and Slip Rings

   a. A slight discoloration of the commutator and slip rings is
normal. Do not attempt to maintain a surface that appears newly
machined. However, the surfaces of the slip rings and the com-
mutator should be smooth and free from pitting and dirt. For
normal cleaning, use a strip of heavy canvas and a dry stick of
wood. Cut the canvas and the stick about the width of the surface
to be cleaned and wrap the canvas over the end of the stick. With
the unit operating, hold the canvas in contact with the surface of
the commutator or slip ring. If the surface is slightly pitted, fold
a piece of No. 0000 sandpaper over the end of the stick and hold
the sandpaper lightly against the surface to be cleaned while the
unit is running. After using the sandpaper, burnish the surface

with the canvas and clean all sand and carbon dust from the unit.

    Caution: When cleaning the commutator or slip rings avoid
coming into contact with live parts of the equipment.

   b. If the surface of the commutator or the slip  rings is badly
pitted or scratched or nicked, remove the armature  and turn it in
for repair to a higher echelon of maintenance. The  armature will
have to be turned down on a lathe.

under the brush in the direction of normal rotation. Lift the
brush and return the sandpaper to its original position. Continue

21. Test Procedures

   a. General. Before making any tests on the rotary converter,
except operational tests, open the de input safety switch and re-

move the fuses. Multimeter TS—352/U may be used for making

continuity tests, checking de voltage, and current. Electrical
Power Test Set TS—914/U should be used for measuring ac voltage
and frequency. Multimeter ME-87/U should be used for testing
the de input circuit.

   b. Fuse Test. If the fuses in the de input circuit have no visible
indication of being open, connect Multimeter ME-87/U across
the de input line; first on the line side and then on the load side
of the fuse holder. If there is voltage on the line side and no
voltage on the load side, one or both of the fuses are either open
or not making contact in the fuse holders. Check fuses and fuse-
holders.

   c. Armature Test.
        (1) Test for ground. First lift the brushes from both the
              commutator and slip rings and block them in raised
              position. With Multimeter TS-—352/U, test between the
               armature shaft and each slip ring. Test between the
               armature shaft and each segment of the commutator. A
              closed circuit reading indicates that the part under test
              is grounded.
       (2) Test for open circuit. Place one of the test prods of the
               multimeter on one of the segments of the commutator.
               Place the other test prod on the adjoining segment. Keep
               the first prod in contact with the one segment and move
               the second prod, from segment to segment, completely
               around the commutator, until a check of. all commutator
              segments has been made. No reading indicates an open
              circuit. If an open circuit is indicated, inspect the sol-
               dered connection on the end of the segment. To test the
              ac circuit, place one test prod on each of the slip rings.
              No reading indicates that the circuit is open.

   d. Capacitor Tests. Before testing a capacitor, disconnect one
end. Make a continuity test between the two terminals of the
capacitor. If continuity is indicated, the capacitor is short-circuited
and must be replaced. The best test for a small capacitor is to
substitute a good capacitor for the one suspected of being faulty.
To be safe, replace any capacitor suspected of causing trouble.

           CHAPTER 4
             THEORY

IH General

   The general theory of the rotary converters is the same for all
models. They are similar in construction to a dc, compound-wound
motor, except that the armature has a second winding from which
110-volt, 60-cycle per second, ac may be obtained. All models of
the equipments functions similarly, but minor differences do exist
between certain models.

23. No-load Operation

   a. Energizing the de side of the rotary converter places line
voltage across the commutator and the shunt field circuits, causing
current to flow in the de armature winding and in the field coils.
The field coils are connected so that every other pole is a north
pole and the remaining poles are south poles. The resulting mag-
netic field reacts to the magnetic field of the armature, causing
the armature to rotate.

    b. An ac voltage is induced in the ac armature winding. The
value of the voltage is determined by the number of turns in the
windings.

    c. The output frequency depends solely on the speed of arma-
ture rotation, which is governed by the magnetic field strength
and the armature voltage. Increasing the resistance in the shunt-
field circuit weakens the magnetic field, causing the armature to
speed up and bring the cemf back to normal. Since the cemf re-
mains unchanged, the output voltage is unchanged. Decreasing
the shunt-field resistance strengthens the magnetic field, reducing
the speed and frequency, and again leaving the output unchanged.

An increase in the applied voltage raises the armature voltage and
hence tends to increase the speed. However, this also strengthens
the magnetic field which tends to oppose the increased armature

speed. Because the per cent of increase in field strength is less
than the per cent of increase in applied voltage, the armature speed
increases. A decrease in applied voltage causes a decrease in speed.

    d. The series-field winding aids starting by adding its effect to
that of the shunt winding. It strengthens the magnetic field and

AGO 3115B             7


---

thereby increases the starting torque. When the rotary converter
is running without load, the series field has practically no effect.

   e. In rotary converters that are provided with interpoles, the
interpole coils are series-connected. These interpole coils reduce
objectionable sparking at the de brushes by reducing the voltage
appearing between the commutator segments at that point.

24. Operation Under Load

   a. When load is connected to the ac output of the rotary con-
verter, the output voltage decreases, even though the input voltage
may remain unchanged. This decrease, caused by the resistance
and reactance of the unit, is greatest when the power factor of
the load is low; then the reactance drop is most effective.

   b. When     the rotary converter is under load, current in the
series field  winding increases. This increase in current in the
series field   winding strengthens the magnetic field, causing a
slight drop   in frequency.

   c. When the rotary converter is under load, current flows in the
armature and the armature becomes a magnet. The result is a
distortion of the magnetic field produced by the field windings.
When the load is of unit power factor (1.0), the field is shifted
so that the neutral point is no longer at the brushes, and sparking
may occur. A load of low power factor produces still another
effect. The lagging component of this load (magnetizing current
for transformers and motors) has a magnetizing effect, oe St:
the field, and causes a rise in frequency.

   d. To summarize the effects of load on the rotary converter; a
load of unity power factor causes a drop in output voltage and a
small drop in frequency and may cause sparking at the brushes.
A load of low power factor produces a large drop in output voltage
and a rise in frequency and may also cause sparking.

31. Replacement of Bearings

   a. Sleeve Bearing Removal. To remove the armature bearings
from rotary converters equipped with sleeve bearings, place the
end bracket on the flat plate of an arbor press, with the hub of
the end bracket facing up. Use a piece of rod or tubing that is
slightly smaller than: the outside diameter of the bearing and
place it between the bearing and the shaft of the press. Be sure
that the ends of the rod or tubing are square and press the bearing
from the end bracket. If the bearing is to be reused, wash it in
cleaning solvent (SD) and wrap it in a clean cloth to protect it
from dust and dirt.

b. Ball Bearing Removal. To remove the armature bearings

from rotary converters equipped with ball bearings, place the

armature in an arbor press so that it is supported only by the

inner race of the bearing. Press the armature shaft out of the

bearing. Be careful not to drop the armature. Wash the bearing

in cleaning solvent (SD) and wrap it in a clean cloth to protect

it from dust and dirt.

Caution: Be sure that the hands are free from dirt and grit

when handling bearings.                 i

c. Sleeve Bearing Installation. Before reinstalling an old bear-

ing or installing a new one, thoroughly clean the bearing housing

and the armature shaft. Use a fine file to remove any burrs or

raised surfaces. Place the end bracket on the flat plate of an

arbor press with the hub of the end bracket resting on the plate.

Set the bearing in place and apply pressure to the bearing, with

the press. Be sure to keep the bearing alined and to keep it from

cocking while pressing it into the housing. Be sure to install any

wicks, felt washers, and plain washers in their correct positions.

After the bearings have been pressed into the bearing housings, in-

stall the end brackets temporarily and line ream the bearings to the

correct size.                \

   d. Ball Bearing Installation, Before reinstalling an old bearing
or installing a new one, thoroughly clean the shaft. Use a fine
file to remove any burrs or raised surfaces. Set the armature in
position with the lower end of the shaft resting on the bottom
plate of the press. Place the bearing in position on the upper end
of the shaft and press it into place. Use a piece of heavy tubing,

slightly larger than the inside diameter of the bearing to keep the
pressure applied to the inner bearing race. Be sure to keep the
bearing in correct alinement while pressing it into place. After
pressing the bearings into place, cover them with a piece of clean
cloth to protect them from dust, grit, and dirt. Be sure to install
all felt seals and washers.

   Caution: Be sure to apply pressure to the inner race of the
bearings only.

32. General Reassembly

   After installing the bearings as instructed in paragraph 31,
reassemble the rest of the rotary converter by reversing the
operations for disassembly. Be sure to replace all washers,
spacers, felt wicks, and grease and oil retainers in the same posi-
tions from which they were removed. Be sure to line up the guide
marks that were made when disassembling. On rotary converters
that have ball bearings, make sure that the bearing housings are
thoroughly clean before reassembling the units. Remake all elec-
trical connections in accordance with the appropriate diagram
(figs. 13 through 17) for the rotary converter that is being
assembled and observe the tags that were attached to the wires
and leads when disassembling. Always assemble the de end of the
rotary converter first and install the armature in the field yoke
with the commutator (dc) end first. Reference to appropriate
illustrations in this manual will aid in assembly.

33. Suppression Component Replacement

    a. General. The rotary converters are suppressed to prevent

interference with nearby radio equipment. Capacitors are in-

stalled in the end brackets; filter assemblies are provided on some

units in which chokes, reactors, and capacitors are installed. Re-

place all suppression components in exactly the same positions

from which they were removed. Be sure that all connections are

tight.  ;

   b. Differences in Filters (figs. 18-21). With the exception of
Rotary Converter PU-141A/U, all/models are provided with
filters. The components of the filters and the values of some of
the components differ from one equipment to another. In some
models an adjustable resistor is used in the filter assembly. The
earlier models were equipped with a resistor with a sliding band
adjustment and later models were equipped with a rheostat.
Reference to figures 18 through 21 will show the physical differ-
ences and references to the appropriate schematic diagrams (figs.
13-17) will show the electrical differences.

install the assemblies in the field yoke. Be sure to install the
narrow shims under the interpoles and the wider shims under the
main poles. Make sure that the leads are positioned properly for
reconnection and fasten the field coils and pole pieces with the
screws that were removed when disassembling.

   6. Pass both the ac and the de leads through the hole in the top
of the field yoke. Bring the ac leads out toward the ac end of
the unit.

   c. Install the end plates on each of the end brackets and screw
both the upper and lower grease plugs into the bearing housings.
Install the de brush holders on the brush holder ring studs. Be
sure that the brush holders are alined with the markings that
were made when disassembling and that the brush slots are radial.
Tighten the squarehead setscrews. Install the brush holder ring
and the capacitors in the de end bracket. Mount the capacitors
to the top screws. Be sure to aline the brush holder ring with the
marks that were made when disassembling and then tighten the
mounting screws.

   d. Install the ac brush holder ring in the ac end bracket with
the brush holders at the top. See that the capacitors are correctly
installed and then install the baffle plate with the grommet hole
at the top.

    e. Mount the de end bracket on the de end of the field yoke.
Be sure to aline the marks that were made when disassembling.
Through the inspection holes, connect the dc field leads, brush
pigtails, and capacitor lugs to the appropriate brush holder termi-
nals. Be sure that all terminal screws are tight.

   f. Remove the protective wrapping from the bearing. See that
the felt seal is on the inner side of the bearing and that the bear-
ing is pressed all of the way onto the armature shaft. Insert the
armature through the field yoke, commutator (dc) end first, and
carefully guide the bearing into the bearing housing in the end
bracket.

    Note. Make sure that the bearing housing is thoroughly clean before in-
serting the bearing.

    g. Mount the ac end bracket assembly on the ac end of the field
yoke. Be sure to aline the marks that were made when disas-
sembling. Pass the ac leads through the grommet hole in the baffle
plate. Connect the ac leads, brush pigtails, and capacitor lugs to
the appropriate brush holder terminals.

38. Final Inspection

   After the equipment has been overhauled, proceed as follows:
   a. Remove the inspection covers and turn the armature by hand
to check freedom of rotation. See that all brush leads and capacitor
leads and connections are secure. Replace the inspection covers.

   b. Remove the filter box cover and inspect all electrical con-
nections within the box to see that they are clean and secure.
On rotary converters that are supplied with shunt field resistors
or rheostats, inspect the contacts to see that they are clean and
not damaged. See that all fastenings are secure and replace the
cover.

   c. Connect the rotary converter to the correct de input voltage
and operate it for about 1 hour, without load. Feel the bearing
housings to see that they do not run hot. Listen for unusual noise
that might indicate trouble.

   d. Place an appropriate load on rotary converter   and operate
it for 2 to 3 hours. Watch for signs of overheating,  sparking at
the brushes, or other unsatisfactory condition.

   e. Inspect all capacitors, bonding washers, and other suppres-
sion components to see that they are properly installed and
making good contact.

   f. Inspect the exterior finish of the rotary converter to see that
there are no bare metal surfaces. Touch up any painted surface
that is scratched or otherwise damaged.

   g. Operate the rotary converter and compare its performance
with the tables in paragraph 5.

                             CHAPTER 6

    SHIPMENT AND STORAGE AND DEMOLITION
                  TO PREVENT ENEMY USE

39. Storage and Shipment

   a. Limited Storage. If the equipment is to be out of use for less
than 30 days, clean it thoroughly, lubricate it, and wrap it in
canvas or other suitable covering. Take every precaution possible
to protect it from moisture. ~

   b. Hatended Storage or Shipment. If the equipment is to be
out of service for 30 days or more, or if it is to be transported a
considerable distance, process it as follows:

       (1) Remove the protective covers and clean the interior of
              the equipment. Replace the covers.

       (2) Lubricate the equipment in accordance with the applic-
              able lubrication order and instructions in paragraph 21
              of this manual.

       (3) Clean the exterior of the equipment and touch up any
              painted surfaces that may have become scratched or
              otherwise damaged. Seal all openings with nonhygro-
              scopic tape.

       (4) If the original packing materials are available, use them

              to repack the equipment. General repacking information
              is usually available at depots. The prime requirement is
              to pack the equipment securely to prevent damage. Use
              sufficient wadding to minimize the effects of severe jolt-
              ing. Be sure that the equipment is protected against
              rain and dampness.

40. Demolition of Materiel to Prevent Enemy Use

   a. General. The following demolition procedures will be ac-
complished, upon order of the commanding officer, to prevent the
enemy from using or salvaging the equipment.

   b. Methods of Destruction. Smash the entire equipment with
sledges, axes, crowbars, and other heavy tools. If on shipboard,
throw the equipment overboard; if on land, bury the equipment
or throw it into a stream or other body of water.

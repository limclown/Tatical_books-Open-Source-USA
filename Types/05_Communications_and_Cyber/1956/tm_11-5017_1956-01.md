1. Scope

   a. This manual contains instructions for the operation, and the
organizational and field maintenance of Output Meters TS—585A/U,
TS-585B/U, and TS—585C/U (figs. 1 and 2).

   b. Official nomenclature followed by (*) is used to indicate all
models of the item of equipment included in this technical manual.
Therefore, Output Meter TS-585(*)/U refers to Output Meters
TS-585A/U, TS-585B/U, and TS-585C/U.

   c. Forward comments on this manual to the Commanding Officer,
The Signal Corps Publications Agency, Fort Monmouth, New Jersey.

   (1) Fill out and forward DA Form 468 (Unsatisfactory Equip-
         ment Report) to Commanding Officer, Signal Equipment

      to Commander, Air Materiel Command, Wright-Patterson
      Air Force Base, Ohio, as prescribed in AF TO 00-35D-—54.

6 (Report of Damaged or Improper Shipment) as prescribed in AR
700-58 (Army); Navy Shipping Guide, Article 1850-4 (Navy); and
AFR 71-4 (Air Force).

9. Preparation for Use

   a. Meter Needle Zero Adjustment.
       (1) If required, set the meter needle on zero (TS—585A/U only)
            by slowly turning the zero-set adjustment screw on the panel
             (fig. 1).
       (2) Read the meter from directly above to avoid parallax error.

   b. Connections.
       (1) Connect a pair of test leads to the binding posts (figs. 1 and 2)
             of the output meter. Connect the other ends of the test leads
             to the output of the equipment to be tested.
      (2) If the equipment is grounded, connect one end of the test
            lead to the grounded output terminal of the grounded equip-
            ment. Connect the other end of the same test lead to the G
            binding post on the output meter.
                 Caution: The TS—585(*)/U is not fused. Do not connect
             the output meter to equipment that delivers more than 5
              watts.

10. Operation

a. Equipment with Known Output Impedance.

(1) Rotate the impedance control knob (figs. 1 and 2) to the

output impedance of the circuit to be tested. Use the

following example:                                    |

Example:

      Equipment impedance is 30 ohms.

      Impedance control knob rotated to 30 in the X1 bracket.

                   Therefore: 30130 ohms.
      (2) Rotate the meter multiplier knob to the 100 position.
      (3) Turn on the equipment to be tested and proceed with its

             operation.
      (4) Adjust the meter multiplier knob to bring the needle on the

            meter to the center, or slightly above the center, of the scale.
      (5) Compute the power output in milliwatts. Use the following

             example:
                Example:
                   Meter multiplier knob points to .1.
                   Meter indication is 15.
                   Therefore: 15X0.1=1.5 milliwatts.

      (6) Read the power output in decibels by adding the decibels
            indication on the meter to the decibels figure for the meter
            multiplier knob position. Use the following examples:
                Example 1:
                   Meter multiplier knob points to +10
                   Meter indication is 15
                    Therefore: 15+10=25 decibles.
                Example 2:
                   Meter multiplier knob points to —10
                   Meter indication is 15
                  Therefore: 15—10=5 decibels.

  b. Hquipment with Unknown Output Impedance.
      (1) Rotate the meter multiplier knob (figs. 1 and 2) to the 100
            position.
      (2) Rotate the impedance control knob clockwise to the 200
            position in the X100 bracket.
       (3) Turn on the equipment under test.
      (4) Slowly rotate the impedance control knob counterclockwise
            until a maximum deflection of the output meter instrument
            is obtained.
      (5) Rotate the meter multiplier knob to bring the meter needle
            near the center of the scale for maximum accuracy.
      (6) Again rotate the impedance control knob very slowly to
            obtain an even greater deflection of the meter needle. Adjust
            the meter multiplier knob, if necessary, for midscale reading.
      (7) The lowest impedance control knob position that produces
            the greatest meter needle deflection is the position at which
            the impedance of the output meter is equal (or matched)
            to the impedance of the equipment under test. Read the
            meter (a(5) or (6) above).
      (8) If the meter indicates zero during the entire operation ((4)-
            (7) above), the output from the equipment under test may
            be insufficient to operate the output meter, or the impedance

           mismatch may be too great for the low output available.

Section |. FIRST ECHELON MAINTENANCE (OPERATOR)

11. Scope of First Echelon Maintenance

   The operator is responsible for the daily and weekly preventive
maintenance of the output meter. General external cleaning, and
tightening of the knob set screws must be performed at specified
intervals. Refer deficiencies to the unit mechanic for correction.

12. Definition of Preventive Maintenance

   Preventive maintenance consists of systematic equipment service
performed at regular intervals in accordance with prescribed methods.
It is intended to minimize breakdown by keeping the equipment
in good operating condition.

13. Use of Preventive Maintenance Form
          (fig. 4)

   a. DA Form 11-238 is a preventive maintenance check list to be
used by the operator as directed.

   6. Items not applicable to the output meter are lined out in figure
4. Paragraph references in the item columns refer to information
pertinent to the particular item.

14. Preventive Maintenance Procedures

   a. Daily.
      (1) Check the general condition of the output meter. Check
             to see that the test leads are not torn, frayed, or otherwise
             damaged. Check the meter glass for cracks and scratches.
            See that both knobs are securely attached to their respective
            shafts.
      (2) Check the knobs for positive action by noting the springiness
            of the detent spring when the knob is rotated. The action
            must be positive and firm. Check for binding, scraping,
            and alignment of the knobs.
      (3) Place the output meter into operation (par. 10) and check
            for normal operation. Use equipment, the power output
            of which is known. Try different impedance settings and
            note the output meter needle deflection. The needle move-
            ment must be free.

15. Scope of Second Echelon Maintenance

   The unit mechanic is responsible for performing the same preventive
maintenance procedures as the operator (pars. 11 and 14). In addi-
tion, the unit mechanic must trouble shoot the output meter and cor-
rect other faults, such as defective wiring that requires soldering, and
also parts replacement. The unit mechanic is authorized tools and
test equipment (par. 16) which will be used to locate and replace
defective parts.

16. Tools and Test Equipment Required

   a. The tools required are contained in Tool Equipment TH-—41.
   6b. The test equipment required is Multimeter TS—297/U.

17. Use of Preventive Maintenance Form
           (fig. 5)

   a. DA Form 11-239 is a preventive maintenance checklist to be
used by the unit mechanic as directed.

   b. Items not applicable to the output meter are lined out on figure
5. Paragraph references in the item columns refer to information
pertinent to the particular item.

18. Preventive Maintenance Procedures

Perform the preventive maintenance procedures as outlined for

the operator (par. 14); in addition, service the znszde of the output

meter as required. Proceed as follows:

a. Remove the output meter chassis from the case.

   6b. Check all mounting and retaining screws.     Hand-tighten if
necessary.

c. Check all parts, supports, and studs for firmness. Look for

worn, cracked, or loose parts. Check the meter glass and case for

damage. Check the moistureproofing and fungiproofing varnish for

adequate surface seal.                  |        ;

   d. If necessary, clean the output meter parts, chassis, and contacts
with Cleaning Compound (Federal Stock No. 7930-395-9542). Dry
thoroughly to remove the residue.

 20. General Theory of Operation

    The TS-585(*)/U is an impedance and power measuring device.
The signal voltage, from the equipment under test, applied to the out-
put meter appears across the impedance-matching network. This ac
voltage is then applied through the meter multiplier circuit to the
rectifier-type audio level meter where it is measured in terms of power
 (milliwatts or decibels). For a more detailed theory of operation, refer
to paragraphs 21 through 25.

21. Impedance Control Circuit

    a. General. ‘The impedance control circuit of the TS-585A/U or the
TS-585B/U (fig. 6) consists of impedance-matching networks Z1 and
Z2 (Z2 represents an autotransformer). The impedance control circuit
of the TS-585C/U (fig. 7) is made up of impedance-matching network
Z1 and autotransformer T1.

   6. Impedance-matching Network Z1. Impedance-matching network
Z\ contains two separate wire-wound resistors. Rotation of the im-
pedance control knob causes the wiper arms to contact the required
amount of resistance through contact studs. The larger of the two re-
sistors is connected across the output meter binding posts; the smaller
resistor is connected in series with the high side of the meter input
circuit. The larger resistor shunts most of the input power to ground;
the smaller resistor reduces the available voltage before it is fed to the
meter multiplier circuit. (The voltage drop decreases with a decrease
in impedance.) The impedance-matching network matches the im-
pedance of the output circuit of the equipment whose power is being
measured. The smaller resistor is in the impedance-matching network
only when the impedance control knob is positioned above 300 ohms on
the TS-585A/U and TS-585B/U or above 1,000 ohms on the TS—
585C/U

   c. Impedance-matching Network 72 (TS-585A/U or TS-585B/U).

hi-lo impedance switch (actually a connection between the innermost
ring and the split ring on top of the impedance control) places the auto-
transformer in shunt with the active portion of the larger resistor of
imdepance-matching network Z1 (6 above). With the imdepance con-
trol knob in a low-imdepance position, the value of the shunt resistor is
lower, and the number of autotransformer turns between the wiper

          (figs. 6 and 7)

   The meter multiplier circuit (E3 in the TS—585A/U or TS-585B/U;

resistors with predetermined values that can be selected. As the

23. Meter Calibration Circuit
   The meter calibration circuit is a bridged T-pad which consists

of series and shunt resistors of fixed values (except for variable
resistor R3). The purpose of the circuit is to reduce the effect of
meter M1 rectifier impedarce changes that result from normally
changing ac voltages which occur during the ac cycle. Variable
resistors R3 enables adjustment of the meter calibration circuit to
allow for slight variations in meter M1 rectifier parts affecting circuit
impedance.

rectifier characteristics, variable resistor R3 provides for adjustment
of the meter calibration circuit immediately preceding the meter
(par 23).

contact surfaces on top of impedance control Z1 and loosened set

30. Consult the symptoms listed in the troubleshooting chart (par.

information in the Probable trouble column in conjunction with the

meter (figs. 8,9, and 10). The wiring diagrams (figs. 13, 14, and 15)
are useful in tracing the wiring between autotransformer T1 and Z2
to impedance control Z1 rings and contacts for purposes of making
the continuity tests.

                     Section Il. CALIBRATION

   Note. The output meter is calibrated during manufacture. Recalibrate only

32. Test Equipment Required for Calibration

   The following test equipment is required for calibrating the output

meter:
      Audio Oscillator TS—382A/U (TM 11-2684)

      Voltmeter Meter ME-30A/U

33. Calibration Procedure

   Calibrate meter M1 as follows:
   a. Remove the output meter chassis from its case and position the
chassis so that meter M1 faces the operator and resistor R3 (figs. 8,
9, and 10) faces away from the operator.
   6. Connect the output of Audio Oscillator TS-382A/U across the
input of the output meter. Rotate the impedance control knob to
the X10 100 position (1,000 ohms). Rotate the meter multiplier
knob to 10. Adjust the TS-382A/U for an output of 1,000 cycles
per second.
   c. Connect Voltmeter Meter ME-30A/U across the output of the
TS-382A/U. Turn on the TS—382A/U and adjust its output until the
ME-30A/U indicates 10 volts.
   d. Adjust variable resistor R3 until meter M1 indicates full scale.

                    Section Ill. FINAL TESTING

34. Percent Accuracy and Meter Scale Divisions

   Output Meter TS—585A/U is accurate to within +10 percent at
half scale; Output Meter TS-—585B/U or TS-585C/U is accurate to
within +5 percent at half scale. (Ten percent is the equivalent of
approximately 2% scale divisions, and 5 percent is the equivalent of
approximately 1% scale divisions on the MILLIWATTS scale.)

36. Testing Impedance Control Circuit

a. Connections. Connect Decade Resistor ZM-16/U, Decade

Resistor TS-679/U, and the output meter in series and across the

output of Audio Oscillator TS-382A/U (fig. 11).

b. Procedure.

(1) Turn on the TS-382A/U and adjust its output frequency

to 10 kilocycles. Adjust for maximum output.

(2) Rotate the impedance control knob on the output meter

to the X.1 x 25 position (2.5 ohms) and adjust the TS—679/U

to 2.5 ohms.                                     ;

(3) Connect Voltmeter Meter ME-30A/U across the output of

the TS-382A/U.

(4) Vary the resistance of the ZM—16/U until the ME-30A/U

indicates 10 volts (fig. 11).

(5) Disconnect the ME-30A/U from across the TS-382A/U and

connect it in turn across the TS—679/U and the TS—585(*)/U.

Read Es and Ex values. The voltage drops across the

TS-679/U and the output meter should be the same. Adjust

the TS-679/U controls, if necessary, until the voltage drops

are the same.                                       |

(6) The panel indication of the TS-679/U should agree with the

output meter impedance control indication to within +5

percent.

(7) Check the remainder of the impedances of the impedance

control circuit by increasing, successively, the impedance

control knob settings and also the settings on the ZM-16/U.

Then proceed as indicated in (5) and (6) above.

38. Repacking for Shipment and Limited Storage

   a. Repack the equipment as shown in figure 3. The original
packing materials may be used.

   b. Pack the equipment securely to prevent damage during transit
or limited storage. Be sure that the equipment is protected from
rain or snow.

39. Methods of Desndciion

   Some or all of the following demolition procedurewisll be used to
prevent the enemy from using or salvaging the output meter and
will be accomplished only upon the order of the commander.

   a. Smash. Smash the carrying case, the meter, the binding posts,
the knobs and electrical and. mechanical parts; use sledges, axes,
hammer or other heavy tools.

   b. Cut. Cut the cables and wires; use axes, hand axes, and machetes.
   c. Burn. Burn all cables, wires, diagrams, and technical manuals;
use gasoline, kerosene, flame throwers, or incendiary grenades.
   d. Explode. If explosives are necessary, use firearms, grenades, or
TNT.
   e. Dispose. Bury or scatter the destroyed parts in slit trenches,
foxholes, or throw them into streams.

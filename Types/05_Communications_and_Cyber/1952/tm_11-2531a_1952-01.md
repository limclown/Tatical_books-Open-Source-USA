          Section |. GENERAL

1. Scope

   a. These instructions are published for the information and guidance
of the personnel to whom this equipment is issued. They contain a
physical description of the equipment, operating instructions, organi-
zational maintenance information, the theory of operation, repair
instructions, repacking instructions for shipment or limited storage,
and instructions for demolishing the equipment to prevent enemy use.
These instructions apply only to Public Address Set AN/TIQ-3A.

   6. Appendix I contains a Jist of current references, including supply
catalogs, technical manuals, and other available publications appli-
cable to the equipment. Appendix II contains an identification table
of parts.

2. Forms and Records

   The following forms will be used for reporting unsatisfactory con-
ditions of Army equipment and in performing preventive maintenance:

   a. DD Form 6, Report of Damaged or Improper Shipment, will be
filled out and forwarded as prescribed in SR 745-45-5 (Army) and
AFR 71-4 (Air Force).

   6. DA Form 468, Unsatisfactory Equipment Report, will be filled
out and forwarded to the Office of the Chief Signal Officer, as prescribed

in SR 700-45-5.
   c. DA AGO Form 11-250, Operator First Echelon Maintenance

Check List for Signal Corps Equipment, Public Address, Recorder,
Reproducers, will be prepared in accordance with instructions on the
back of the form.

   d. DA AGO Form 11-251, Second and Third Echelon Maintenance
Check List for Signal Corps Equipment, Public Address, Recorder,
Reproducer, will be prepared in accordance with instructions on the
back of the form.

   e. Use other forms and records as authorized.

Loudspeakers LS-104/TIQ-3. Handset H-33D/PT, or a monitor

15. Unusual Conditions

    Public Address Set AN/TIQ-3A may be operated under unusual
conditions of severe dust, moisture, and temperature without injury
to the equipment. However, if operation under unusual conditions is
to be maintained for long periods, certain precautions must be taken.
Refer to paragraphs 16, 17, and 18 for information covering operation
under specific unusual conditions.

16. Operation in Arctic Climates

    Condensation will form inside the public address set when it is
exposed alternately to low and high temperatures. Maintain as
constant a temperature as is possible in the storage of the equipment.
Refer to TB SIG 66 for additional winter operational information.

17. Operationin Desert Climates

    Preventing the entry of dust and sand is a major problem in desert
areas. Under severe conditions it may be necessary to improvise
covers to protect the equipment from dust and sand. Consult
TB SIG 75 for additional desert operational information.

18. Operation in Tropical Climates

   The public address set has been moistureproofed and fungiproofed
adequately. Excessive moisture between high voltage and ground
may result in the formation of leakage paths and voltage discharges.
Damage to the varnish film must be repaired immediately upon dis-
covery. Cover the equipment whenever feasible to avoid drenching
during tropical downpours. Cover it tightly after use to conserve
internal heat and to reduce the possibility of moisture condensation
during the cooler nights.

                Section Ill. TROUBLE SHOOTING

38. General Trouble-Shooting Information

   When troubles occur, the repairman must locate and correct them
as rapidly as possible.. By proper use of the following information,
the operator can isolate the trouble quickly and thus save time.
Consult the schematic diagrams for resistance and capacitance data.
Refer to the illustrations of components with reference symbols which
aid in locating and identifying parts.

   a. Tubes. Before setting up an elaborate trouble-shooting procedure,
replace the tubes with known good tubes to eliminate possible trouble
from this source.

   6. Visual Inspection. Make a thorough visual inspection in an-
attempt to locate the trouble. Check for loose connections, charred
insulation, and charring or discoloration of resistors or capacitors.
If inspection fails to locate the trouble, make voltage and resistance
analyses of the components or circuits suspected of failure.

39. Voltage Measurements

   a. General. Normal voltage values are an aid to the repairman,
because most troubles either result from abnormal voltages or produce
abnormal voltages. Voltage measurements always are made between
two points in a circuit, and the circuit need not be interrupted.

      (1) Complete information on normal operating voltages is given
             in the voltage-resistance charts (pars. 44 and 45). Unless
             otherwise specified, these voltages are measured between the
             indicated points and ground.

      (2) Always begin by setting the voltmeter on the highest range
             so that it will not be damaged by overloading. Then, if it
             is necessary (to obtain increased accuracy), set the voltmeter
             to a lower range.

42


---

   b. Voltmeter Loading. It is essential that the voltmeter resistance
be at least 10 times as great as the resistance of the circuit across
which the voltmeter is placed. If the voltmeter resistance is too
low, it will indicate a lower voltage than the actual circuit voltage.

AO. Resistance Measurements

   a. Normal Resistance Values. A fault often will show up as a change
in the resistance values. To assist in the localization of such faults,
trouble-shooting data include the normal resistance values as measured
at the tube sockets and at the resistor and capacitor board terminal
points. The normal resistance values at any point can be determined .
by referring to the resistance values shown in the schematic diagrams
 (figs. 22 and 23), in the voltage-resistance charts (pars. 44 and 45),
and by use of the resistor color code (fig. 25).

    b. Precautions. Before making any resistance measurements, turn
off the power. An ohmmeter is essentially a low-range voltmeter and
battery. If the ohmmeter is connected to a circuit which already
has voltage in it, the meter needle may be knocked off scale, and the
meter movement may be damaged. Capacitors always must be dis-
 charged before resistance measurements are made to prevent damage
 to the meter movement.

    c. Correct Use of Low and High Ranges. When checking the circuit
 continuity, set the ohmmeter at its lowest range. If the medium
 or high range is used, the pointer may indicate zero ohms, even if
 the resistance is as high as 500 ohms. When checking high resistances
 or measuring the leakage resistance of capacitors or cables, use the
 highest range. If a low range is used, the pointer may indicate in-
finite ohms, even though the actual resistance is less than a megohm.

    d. Parallel Resistance Connections.
        (1) Ina parallel circuit, the total resistance is less than the small-
              est resistance in the circuit. This is important to remember
              when shooting trouble with the aid of a schematic diagram.
        (2) If a resistance value is found to be less than expected, study
              the schematic to be certain that there are no circuits in
              parallel with the resistor that is being measured. Disconnect
              one termigal from the resistor and measure it again.

 41. Capacitor Tests

    a. Capacitor Defects. It often is necessary and desirable to check
 capacitors for leakage and open or short circuits. Although open
 circuits sometimes occur in paper-type capacitors because of the metal
 terminal tap pulling away from the tinfoil plates, this trouble is rare.
 The usual trouble in capacitors is a short circuit or leakage caused by
 the breakdown of the dielectric between the plates. This usually
 applies only to capacitors of the tinfoil-paper or mica type, since
 electrolytic capacitors normally show some leakage.

                                                                                                          43


---

    b. Open Capacitor. A capacitor suspected of being open can be
checked by shunting a good capacitor across it. In audio circuits,
the length of the leads of the test capacitor is not critical.

   c. Kick Indication. A high-resistance voltohmmeter may be used
to check for leakage in a paper or mica capacitor by means of the
kick indication. 'To make this test, disconnect one end of a capacitor
from its circuit. Set the ohmmeter to its highest range, and place
the ohmmeter leads across the capacitor. A good capacitor should
cause the needle to kick up the scale and fall back to infinity. A
leaky capacitor will cause the needle to remain at some point on—
the scale other than infinity. An open capacitor will result in no
deflection of the needle. The lower the capacitance, the smaller the
deflection on the ohmmeter scale. The higher the capacitance, the
greater the deflection on the ohmmeter scale (if the capacitor is
normal). Capacitors lower than 50,000 uf (micromicrofarads) usu-
ally do not produce a measurable kick.

   d. Color Codes. 'The capacitor color codes (fig. 24) may be used to
check the rated capacitor values of the capacitors in the equipment
against the values shown on the schematic diagram.

42. Trouble-Shooting Hints

   a. Effect of Low Screen or Plate Voltage. Low screen or plate volt-
age decreases the amplification of the stage. This type of fault can
be located by resistance and voltage measurements.

   b. Checking Cathode Voltage. When checking cathode voltage, a
false reading may be obtained even if the cathode resistor actually
is open, because the resistance of the voltmeter acts as the cathode
resistor. Make a resistance check of this resistor with the power
turned off.

   c. Effect of Shorted Screen-Bypass Capacitor. A shorted screen-
bypass capacitor will result in the output of the stage dropping to
a very small value. It can be localized rapidly by either a voltage
or a resistance measurement. After replacement of the capacitor,
check the screen resistor for overload damage.

   d. Effect of Open Coupling Capacitor. When a coupling capacitor
between the plate of one stage and the control grid of the next stage
opens, very little or no signal appears on the control grid of the
second tube, even though there is a high signal voltage at the plate
of the first tube.

   e. Effect of Shorted Coupling Capacitor. When the coupling capaci-
tor between the plate of one stage and the control grid of the next
stage shorts out or leaks, a weak signal, or no signal, appears on
the grid. The leaking, or shorted capacitor, allows a positive po-

             Section IV. REPAIRS

46. General

   a. Kailure of Part Caused by Another Defective Part. When a
defective part, such as a burned-out resistor or transformer, is
located, check the associated circuit thoroughly to determine whether
this condition is caused by another faulty part. For example, a
shorted capacitor may destroy a transformer or a resistor in the
associated circuit. If a transformer or a resistor is replaced without
replacing the defective capacitor which caused the breakdown, the
new part will be burned out in the same manner.

   b. Soldering. It is important to make good solder joints; poorly
soldered connections can cause trouble which is difficult to find. Use
the soldering iron carefully so that insulation on wiring and other
adjacent parts are not damaged by overheating. Use only enough
solder to make a good joint. Do not permit drops of solder to fall
inside the chassis; they may cause short circuits and damage the
equipment. ‘Tag each lead to insure that the part can be replaced
correctly and quickly.

47. Fuse Replacement

   Remove fuse F1 or F2 as follows:
   a. Turn the knurled fuseholder counterclockwise until the holder
can be removed.
   6b. Remove the defective fuse from the holder and replace it with a
good one.
   c. Reinsert the holder in the chassis fuse receptacle and turn it
clockwise until it is handtight.

48. Pilot Lamp Replacement

   Replace pilot lamps E3 or E4 as follows:
   a. Remove the knurled holding ring of the pilot lamp assembly
by turning it counterclockwise. To remove the pilot lamp, push
the bulb into the socket, turn it slightly to the left, and remove it.
   b. Push the new bulb into the socket and turn it slightly clockwise
until it is locked in place and replace the knurled holding ring.

49. Vacuum Tube and Glow Lamp Replacement

   To gain access to the tubes and glow lamps, it is necessary to
remove the amplifier and the control unit from their cases. Remove
the three machine screws located on each side of the front panels
of the amplifier and control unit and pull the units from their cases.

   a. Release the tube clamps which secure the tubes and lift the
tubes straight out of their sockets.

   b. To remove glow lamp E1 or E2, press down on the lamp, twist

   Caution: Note the number on the replacement tube and check

50. Audio Frequency Amplifier AM-700/TIQ-3

   If the amplifier is not functioning properly, check the voltages
at the tube sockets of tubes V1, V2, V4, V5, C6, V7, V8, and V9 with
a d-c voltmeter. The correct voltages are shown in the voltage and
resistance charts (pars. 44 and 45). If proper voltages are not
obtained at any tube, remove the power and check the circuit of the
tube and all its components with an ohmmeter for short or open
circuits.

51. Public Address Set Control C-1090/TIQ-3

   If the control unit is not functioning properly, check the voltages
at the tube sockets of tubes V3, V10, and V11 with a d-c voltmeter.
The correct voltages are shown in the voltage and resistance charts
(pars. 44 and 45). If proper voltages are not obtained at any tube,
remove the power and check the circuit of the tube and all of its
components with an ohmmeter for short or open circuits.

52. Handset H-33D/PT

   a. To test the microphone (transmitter) of the handset, apply
an ohmmeter across terminals C and D of connector P19. A reading
of 75 to 250 ohms should be obtained, depending on atmospheric
conditions and the position of carbon granules in the transmitter.
The reading should vary with the movement of the handset. If an
incorrect reading is obtained, turn in the handset for exchange or
repair.

   b. To test the continuity of the receiver of the handset, check across
terminals A and B of connector P19 with an ohmmeter. A reading of
approximately 120 ohms should be obtained. If the reading is incor-
rect, turn the handset in for exchange or repair.

53. Loudspeaker LS—104/TIQ-3

    In general, it will be found that if trouble does develop in the loud-

speaker, it will be necessary to replace either the complete diaphragm

head assembly or the line-matching tr ansiormer T8. To test the

loudspeaker, proceed as follows:                    ree

    a. Unsolder the two wires from the driver unit, noting the solder lug

to which each wire is connected.

    b. Using the low-resistance scale on the ohmmeter, check across the

two terminal lugs for a continuity reading of approximately 15 ohms.

While this is being done, a click should be heard.

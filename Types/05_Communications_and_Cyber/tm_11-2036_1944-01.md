5. GENERAL.
   a. The current-flow test set provides a means for regulating and

for measuring the values of current applied to the windings of relays
and similar electrical apparatus, for testing and adjusting purposes.
This usage involves the connection of the test set at a particular point
in the electrical circuit of the apparatus under test. It also involves
connecting to the test set in a particular way, and usually, connecting
battery or ground supply to the test set. It may require preliminary
preparation, such as the blocking of other relays operated or non-
operated or closing or opening particular contacts in the circuit under
test.

   b. Information of this nature, which is necessary for current-flow-
ing apparatus, is usually found in the technical manuals pertaining to
the equipment, together with the current values on which each relay
(or similar apparatus) is to be checked for proper performance and
the values to which the equipment must be readjusted. In some cases,
this information is provided in circuit requirement tables (fig. 4)
associated with the schematic circuit drawings. When circuit require-
ment tables are the source of this information, it will be found under
the following column headings:

9. CONNECTION TO RELAY UNDER TEST.
   Paragraph 8, covering test set preparation and connections, includes

instructions for the connection of cords to the test set for the purpose
of connecting to the relay to be tested. These cords must be con-
nected to the proper relay winding terminals or other points in the
circuit in accordance with the technical manual covering the equip-
ment, or as indicated in the circuit requirement tables. In the latter
case, the columns CONN BATT and CONN GRD under CIRCUIT
PREPARATION show this information. Follow the instructions in
figures 5, 6, 7, or 8. Note that CONN BATT indicates the point of
connection to a relay winding for a test cord which will supply bat-
tery through the test set circuit, likewise CONN GRD indicates point
of connection to a relay winding for a test cord which will supply
ground through the test set circuit.

10. RELAY PERFORMANCE REQUIREMENTS.
   The requirements for performance of a relay when a specified cur-

rent value is passed through its winding are given in the technical
manuals applying to the equipment or in circuit requirement tables.
The following general definitions explain the electrical requirements
usually specified.

   a. Operate (O). A relay is said to operate if, when current is con-
nected to its winding, the armature moves sufficiently from its unop-
erated position to cause all normally open contacts to close and all
normally closed contacts to open.

   b. Nonoperate (NO). A relay is said to nonoperate if, when current
is connected to its winding, the armature does not move from the
unoperated position. On some types of relays the armature may move
slightly, but not sufficiently to close the open contacts or to cause the
closed contacts to become unreliable.

   c. Hold (H). A relay is said to hold if, after the relay has operated
and the current is reduced abruptly, the armature “holds” or does not
move from the operated position.

   d. Release (R). A relay is said to release if the armature moves from
its operated to its unoperated position and all normally open contacts —
are open and all normally closed contacts are closed. Current flow
requirements for release specify the current on which a relay must
release after it has been operated. This involves reduction of the cur-
rent from an “operate,” “hold,” or “soak” value to a steadily main-
tained “release” value, without interruption of the current.

   (e) ““Soak’”’ Requirements. For certain relays, it is necessary to es-
tablish a definite magnetic condition in their cores before they are
tested or readjusted to their electrical requirements. To obtain this
magnetic condition, a comparatively heavy current is passed through

11. TESTING PROCEDURES.
   Having connected the test set to the relay or other apparatus to be

tested and when required, to a battery or ground supply as covered
in paragraphs 8 and 9, the resistances in the test set should be adjusted
by means of the resistance control knobs and keys, until the required
amount of current is established through the relay winding. The pro-
cedure to be followed in the operation of the test set, when testing
a relay for its “operate” (O) requirement is described in paragraph
12. This same general procedure is followed when applying the other
types of current-flow requirements or combinations of requirements,
except as modified by paragraph 13. The procedure to be followed
when a “soak” current is specified in the current-flow requirements of
a relay is explained in paragraph 13.

12. PROCEDURE WHEN TESTING FOR “OPERATE” (OQ).
   a. Choice of Circuit Path. Either circuit path 1 or 2 may be used.

Circuit path 2 has a higher maximum resistance than path 1, there-
fore it is possible to establish lower current values with circuit path 2.

   b. Setting of Keys. Check the setting of the knobs and keys of the
test set before starting the test.
(1) Key REV should be in its operated, that is, its depressed posi-
tion and should remain in this position during the test unless other-
wise specified in the technical manual or circuit requirements table.
(2) The meter scale change key should be in its 150 MA position.

     CAUTION: In order to avoid damage to the meter movement,
     do not change the position of the meter scale change key ex-
     cept under the following conditions: When the meter reading
     is less than 75 MA (below the half way mark when using the
     150 MA scale) the meter scale key may be changed to its
     75 MA position, in order to obtain a more accurate reading.
     When the meter reading on either the 0-150 MA or 0-75 MA
     scale is less than 15 MA, the meter scale key may be changed
     to its 15 MA position for a more accurate reading.
     Always return the scale change key to the 150 MA position
     before reducing the resistance in the circuit.

(3) Turn all resistor knobs and keys to their MAX RES position (full
counterclockwise position for 1 and 2 knobs).

     CAUTION: To avoid excessive current through the meter,
     always start with maximum resistance in the circuit paths. To
     obtain the required current reading, the resistance should be
     reduced gradually, so that there will not be a sharp increase
     in the current which may exceed the full scale value of the

   c. Adjustment of Current Value. Operate the control key associated
with the path to be used; adjust the test set resistance control until
the meter indicates the current value specified for the test. Make
preliminary adjustments of resistance with the COARSE rheostat
knob, keeping the FINE rheostat knob at its MAX RES position. If
an approximately correct meter reading can be obtained with the
COARSE knob, the FINE knob is then used to obtain a more accurate
value of current. If the required current reading cannot be obtained
with the COARSE and FINE rheostats, return both rheostat knobs
to their MAX RES position before reducing the resistance by use of
the twist keys. Take out resistance with the twist keys one step at a
time, until the value of current specified for the test can be obtained
within the range of the rheostats.

up on the circuit path being used, the test for the “operate” require-
ment (when no “soak”’ is specified) is made in the following manner.
Release the circuit control key (1 or 2) to its center position; this
will open the circuit through the relay connected for test so that the
relay is unoperated; then reoperate the control key to apply the
current through the relay winding, and observe the performance of
the relay. The relay should “operate” (O) as defined in paragraph
10a.

the procedure are necessary to properly apply some requirements
or combinations of requirements. For example, when the relay being
 tested must meet several electrical requirements it is preferable and
in some cases necessary to use both circuit paths 1 and 2 of Test Set
 I-181, setting up and applying the current values in a certain way.
 Reference should be made to the following information.

   a. “Soak” Requirement Using “Soak” Circuit Path. The electrical re-
quirements for a relay may specify that the tests for performance of
the relay must be preceded by a “soak” (par. 10e). In general, when
a “soak” is specified for any type of WE relay other than a WE Y-type
relay or a polar-type relay (par. 27), it is satisfactory to use the non-
adjustable current value provided by the “soak” path of Test Set
I-181. Apply this “soak” current by operating the SOAK control key
for 1 or 2 seconds; then release the key, and within 1 or 2 seconds
apply the associated electrical requirement to the relay. For applying
“soak” current to WE Y-type or polar-type relays, see subparagraph
b below.

   b. ““Soak’”” Requirement Using Circuit Path 1 (Regulated ‘Soak’ Cur-
rent Value). (1) When a “soak” is required for WE Y-type or polar-
type relays, the “soak” path of Test Set I-181 cannot be used, since
the “soak” current applied to these relays must be one of the exact
value specified in the electrical requirements. Path 1 or path 2 of the
test set therefore must be used since the current applied over either
of these paths may be regulated as to value.
(2) Establish the value of current required for the test for perform-
ance of the relay (either “operate,” ‘“nonoperate,’ 99 6e “release,” or
“hold”’) on circuit path 2; then establish the current value specified
for the “soak” on path 1. To apply the “soak,” operate the path 1 con-
trol key for 1 or 2 seconds, then release the control key, and within
1 or 2 seconds apply the associated electrical requirement by operating
the path 2 control key.

   c. ‘‘Nonoperate (NO)’”’ Requirements. The procedure for testing a re-
lay for “operate” is given in paragraph 12. The procedures are the
same when applying a nonoperate test. As will be seen from the defini-
tion of “nonoperate” paragraph 10b, the relay must be in an unop-
erated (released) position just before applying the “nonoperate’”’
value of current. Therefore, when a “soak” current value is specified
to precede the application of “nonoperate” current, apply the “soak”
momentarily, then allow the relay to release, after which immediately
apply the “nonoperate” value of current. The relay should not op-
erate. Do not apply the “operate” or “nonoperate” test with circuit
path 1, while maintaining the current value for “hold” on path 2 as
covered in paragraph 13d, since this will prevent the relay from re-
leasing to its unoperated position.

    d. “Hold (H)” or “Release (R)’’ Requirements. When a hold or re-
lease requirement (par. 10c and 10d) must be met, the current
through the relay winding must be reduced directly from an “operate”
or “soak” value to a steadily maintained “hold” or “release” value
without interrupting the current or reducing it below the current
value specified for the test being made (“hold” or “release’”’). There-

and applied over circuit path 1, the current on circuit path 2 forms

path 2 control key will affect the value of current which will be
applied by operation of path 1 control key and will make it necessary
to readjust path 1 resistors. This should be kept in mind when setting
up and applying, in turn, different values of current with path 2
(such as “operate,” “nonoperate,” “hold,” “release”) preceded each
time by a “soak” or other current value applied over path 1.

14. USE AS A MILLIAMMETER.
   Test Set I-181 may be used as a milliammeter, by adding a strap

across binding posts T and R and using binding posts BAT and GRD,
or the tip and ring of jack BG as meter terminals. The “2” circuit of
the set is the most convenient for this use, since control key “2”
may be operated to, and left in its operated position to provide con-
tinuity through the test set and meter. The “2-COARSE” and
‘“2-FINE” rheostats must be turned to their extreme clockwise posi-
tions, and both the 0-7500-15000 and the 0-15000-30000 twist keys
turned to their 0 positions, in order to eliminate resistance in series
with the meter within the test set. The resistance of the test set used
as a milliammeter is approximately 15 ohms as a 15-milliampere
meter; 10 ohms as a 75-milliampere meter, and 9 ohms as a 150-
milliampere meter. These values include the resistance of the meter
 M1 and the fuse Fl. The resistance of the fuse is approximately
7.9 ohms.

 15. GENERAL.
    A schematic of the test set circuit is shown in figure 2. The circuit

is arranged with three separate control paths, so that three separate
current values can be applied to the apparatus to which the set is
connected, without changing the test connections. These three paths
are path 1, path 2, and the SOAK path. The value of current applied
by using path 1 or path 2 can be regulated and read on the meter. The
SOAK path is not adjustable as to current value. It provides a fixed
value of ‘‘soak” or heavy current.

 16. PATHS 1 AND 2.
   Paths 1 and 2 both include the milliammeter, a rheostat, poten-

tiometer and key-controlled resistors by means of which the amount
of current can be regulated. Rheostats R7 and R8 provide a “coarse”
series resistance adjustment.:Potentiometers Pl and P2 are used as
“fine” series resistance adjustment rheostats. The fixed resistors R5
and R6 in path 1 are controlled by key K5. In path 2, fixed resistors
R1 and R2 are controlled by key K1, and R3 and R4 are controlled by
key K2. The maximum total series resistance provided in path 1 is
11,500 ohms; in path 2, 55,500 ohms. Paths 1 and 2 are both protected
by the 1/8-amp fuse F1 and are connected to the milliammeter by
way of the meter-scale change key K4. Operation of key K4 selects
the scale range of the meter. The meter has three scale ranges; 0-150
MA, 0-75 MA, and 0-15 MA.

17. SOAK PATH.
   The “soak” circuit path is not connected through the meter or fuse

and is not provided with adjustable resistors. A 50-ohm protective
resistor, consisting of the 200-ohm resistors R10, R11, R12, and R13
in parallel (fig. 2), is provided in the SOAK path. Except for the
voltage drop resulting from this resistor, the full battery voltage can
be applied to an external circuit connected to this circuit path. This
is sometimes required as a means of applying a “soak” or saturating
current as part of the electrical requirements of certain relays or
other apparatus. Paragraph 10e explains “soak” requirements more
fully.

18. CIRCUIT CONTROL KEYS.

   Current is connected over path 1, path 2, and the SOAK path by
operation of the circuit control keys, which are designated in figure
2 as K3 and K6. Keys K3 and K6 are three-position keys and are non-
operated when in their normal or middle position. Key K3, when
operated in either direction, connects the current through circuit
path 2. Key K3 is locking in one of its operated positions and non-
locking in the other. Key K6 connects the current through circuit
path 1 when operated in one direction and through the “soak”
circuit path when operated in the other direction. Key K6 is non-
locking in both operated positions.

connects to binding post BAT. Plug PL-51 or equal (WE No. 110 or
No. 310) may be connected to this jack.

26. MOISTUREPROOFING AND FUNGIPROOFING.
   a. General The operation of Signal Corps equipment in tropical

areas where temperature and relative humidity are extremely high
requires special attention. The following items represent problems
which may be encountered in operation:
(1) Resistors fail.
 (2) Electrolytic action takes place in resistors causing eventual
break-down.
(3) Hook-up wire and cable insulation break down. Fungus growth
accelerates deterioration.
(4) Moisture forms electrical leakage paths on terminal boards and
insulating strips, causing flash-overs and crosstalk.
(5) Moisture provides leakage paths between battery terminals.

   b. Treatment. A moistureproofing and fungiproofing treatment has
been devised which, if properly applied, provides a reasonable degree
of protection against fungus growth, insects, corrosion, salt spray,
and moisture. The treatment involves the use of a moisture- and
fungi-resistant varnish applied with a spray gun or brush. Refer to
TB SIG 13, Moistureproofing and Fungiproofing Signal Corps Equip-
ment, for a detailed description of the varnish-spray method of mois-
tureproofing and fungiproofing.

     CAUTION: Varnish spray may have toxic effects if inhaled. To
     avoid inhaling spray, use respirator if available; otherwise,
     fasten cheesecloth or other cloth material over nose and mouth.

   c. Step-by-step Instructions for Treating Test Set I-181.
(1) PREPARATION.

   (a) Make all repairs and adjustments necessary for proper opera-
tion of the equipment.

   (b) Clean all dirt, dust, rust, fungus, oil, grease, etc., from the
equipment to be processed.
(2) DISASSEMBLY.

   (a) Remove cover from case by releasing catches.
   (b) Remove four screws from front panel and lift chassis from
case.
   (c) Remove three knobs from key switches.
   (d) Remove twelve screws holding three key switches to front
panel, allowing switches to hang free on attached wires.
   (e) Unlace and separate wires.
(3) MASKING. Cover the following parts with masking tape:
   (a) Contacts on key jack (fig. 9(1)).

   (b) Contacts on variable resistors (fig. 9(2)).
    (c) Contacts on key switches (fig. 9(3)).
(4) DRYING. Place equipment in an oven or under heat lamps and
dry for 2 to 3 hours at 160° F. Do not exceed 160° F. If wax should

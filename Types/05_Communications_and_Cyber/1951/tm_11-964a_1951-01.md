3. Description

   a. Rectifier RA-91-B (fig. 1) is a full-wave, selenium-disk rectifier
designed to deliver a d-c (direct-current) output suitable for charging
lead-acid storage batteries from a 105- to 125- or 210- to 250-volt, 50-

to 60-cycle, a-c (alternating-current) source.

   b. The complete unit is inclosed in a metal case which is designed
to be mounted in Panel BD-132. The over-all dimensions are 1914
inches wide, 12 inches deep, and 2114 inches high; the weight is 100

pounds.

           Section Il. CONTROLS AND INSTRUMENTS

11. Circuit Breakers

             (fig. 2)

    a. Two magnetic-type circuit breakers are provided for control
of the input and output circuits. They operate automatically if an
overload develops in either circuit. The a-c INPUT circuit breaker
automatically breaks the a-c line feeding the charger transformer if
the input current exceeds 5 amperes. The d-c OUTPUT circuit
breaker automatically breaks the d-c charging circuit if the charging
current exceeds 20 amperes. The purpose of the circuit breakers is to
prevent damage to the charger in the event of an overload. They also
act as on-off switches for the equipment.

    6b. The a-c circuit breaker is located on the left of the panel; the d-c
circuit breaker is located on the right of the panel. The circuit
breakers are labeled a-c INPUT and d-c OUTPUT. Placing the a-c
INPUT circuit breaker in the ON position sets the circuit breaker
mechanism for tripping in the event of an overload and also completes
the a-c line to the transformer primary which places the charger in
operation. Placing the d-c OUTPUT circuit breaker in the ON posi-
tion sets the circuit breaker mechanism for tripping and also completes
the battery-charging circuit. Placing either circuit breaker in the OFF
position opens the appropriate circuit and cuts off the a-c input or
disconnects the load.

12. Control Switches
          (fig. 2)

    a. COARSE CONTROL SWITCH. This is an eleven-position rotary
switch connected across half of the secondary of the transformer.
The switch enables the operator to select the proper number of turns
in the transformer secondary to adjust the current in the battery-
charging circuit. The switch is used to obtain a rough adjustment of
current. Use the COARSE control switch to obtain a rough adjust-
ment of output current; use the FINE control switch (6b below) to ob-
tain an exact adjustment of output current. Switch position 1 taps
the fewest number of turns in the transformer secondary and provides
the maximum charging current; switch position 11 taps the largest
number of turns and provides the minimum charging current.

    b. FINE CONTROL SwITtcH. This switch is identical in operation
to the COARSE control switch. It is used to provide finer control of

the charging current than it is possible to provide with the COARSE

control alone.

13. Ammeter
          (fig. 2)

  The ammeter indicates the battery-charging current in amperes.
All battery-charging current flows through the ammeter which is
connected in series with the common return circuit of the full-wave
rectification circuit. The ammeter range is 0 to 15 amperes; as the d-c
output is fused at 20 amperes, there is little danger of meter burnout.

14 Pilot Light

            (fig. 2)

  The pilot light is connected across the a-c input to the transformer
on the a-c INPUT circuit breaker side of the circuit. It is energized
when the circuit breaker closes the circuit to the transformer primary.
The lamp glows and indicates that current is flowing in the primary
circuit of the charger. This does not necessarily mean that the charger
is operating; the lamp may glow even if no load is placed upon the
d-c OUTPUT circuit. Do not assume that the glowing of the lamp
means that the rectifier is delivering current to the battery under
charge. Check the ammeter indication to determine whether current

is flowing through the battery.

  Section Ill. OPERATION UNDER USUAL CONDITIONS

15. Preliminary Procedures

   a. Place each circuit breaker in the OFF position.
   b. Turn each control switch to position 11.
   c. Check all external connections carefully.
   d. Be certain that the batteries are connected correctly.

(Refer to TM 11-2064 and TM 9-2857 for the proper method of con-

necting batteries for charging.)

16. Starting

  a. Place each circuit breaker in the ON position. (The pilot light

will glow if the primary circuit is continuous.)

   b. Turn the coarse adjustment control switch counterclockwise from
position 11. The ammeter should indicate a low charging rate. If
the ammeter does not register any current, make sure that the d-c
circuit breaker is closed. The ammeter should register a flow of cur-

rent if the secondary circuit (par. 28b) is continuous.

     c. Adjust the charging current to the desired rate (refer to TM 9-
 2857) by operating the FINE control switch counterclockwise from
 position 11. The charging rate must never exceed 12 amperes.

 17. Continuous Charging with Rectifier RA-91-B

     The purpose of continuous charging (trickle charging) is to furnish
 a steady charging current 24 hours a day, which will be adequate to
replace the drain on the batteries used in telephone central office sets.

     a. CHARGING RATE. Refer to TM 9-2857 for complete information
 on approved charging methods and correct charging rates for lead-
acid storage batteries.

    b. CHECKING CONDITION OF CHARGE. The methods listed below
may be used to determine whether or not the batteries in the system
are fully charged.

         (1) Use a hydrometer to measure the specific gravity of the elec-
                 trolyte. Be certain that the hydrometer is equipped with a
                thermometer; if this is not the case, measure the temperature
                 of the electrolyte with a thermometer at the same time the
                 specific gravity reading is made. Refer to TM 9-2857 and
                 TM 11-480 for correct values of specific gravity.

        (2) Connect a d-c voltmeter momentarily across the battery
                 terminals. The reading across each cell should be between
                 2.3 to 2.5 volts when the battery is fully charged. A reading
                 of 1.75 volts indicates that the battery is completely dis-
                charged.

    c. IMPROPER CHARGING. If the batteries do not become fully
charged after a reasonable period of time (24 hours), check the battery
for dead or defective cells (see TM 9-2857). If the battery is gassing
and bubbling violently and the temperature of the liquid in the cells
rises, the charging rate is too high. Reduce the charging rate
accordingly.

    d. DAILY BATTERY CHECK. Check the condition of each battery
in the system at least once each day to be certain that it is receiving
the proper charge and that the liquid level-in each cell is correct. Do
not let the battery run down because the charging rate is too low, and
do not ruin the batteries by overcharging.

18. Intermittent (Rapid) Charging with Rectifier RA-91-B

   Rectifier RA-91-B may be used for intermittent (rapid) charging
when it is necessary to charge batteries as quickly as possible. Refer
to TM 9-2857 and TM 11-430 for the procedure to be followed.

Section IV. OPERATION UNDER UNUSUAL CONDITIONS

19. General

  Rectifier RA-91-B may be operated under unusual climatic con-
ditions without injury to the equipment. If, however, operation
under unusual conditions is to be maintained for long periods, certain
precautions must be taken. Refer to paragraphs 20 through 22 for

information covering operation under unusual conditions.

90. Operation in Arctic Climates

   Condensation will form inside the rectifier if the unit is exposed to
alternate low and high temperatures. Maintain the temperature in
the room or shelter which houses the rectifier at as constant a level as
possible. The internal resistance of the batteries being charged in-
creases with a decrease in temperature. Refer to TB SIG 66 for pre-

 cautions necessary under arctic conditions.

 91. Operation in Desert Climates

   Take precautions against the entry of sand and dust into the equip-
 ment. Improvise a suitable cover and place it completely around the
 rectifier whenever it is taken out of operation. Where feasible, run
 the rectifier at a lower rate to reduce the operating temperature.
 Refer to TB SIG 75 for additional precautions for desert operation.

 292. Operation in Tropical Climates

   Although the rectifier is a low-voltage, high-current device, the
 presence of moisture and mold on the parts may impair the operation
 of the equipment. The varnish applied to the parts during the mois-
 tureproofing and fungiproofing treatments will protect against mois-
 ture and mold attack. Damage to the varnish film must be repaired
 as quickly as possible. As soon as the rectifier is shut down for the
  day, cover it thoroughly so that no moisture will be drawn into the
  equipment when the rectifier is taken out of service. Protect the
  equipment against sudden tropical downpours. Refer to TB SIG
  13 and TB SIG 72 for additional tropical operational information.

28. General
          (fig. 5)

    The rectifier consists of four basic parts; the transformer (T1), the
circuit breakers (K1 and K2), the selenium rectifier stacks (CR1 and
C2), and the filter reactor (L1).

    ad. TRANSFORMER. The function of the transformer is to provide
the voltage and current required for the rectifier stacks to produce a
d-c output for the charging circuit. The transformer also permits
control of the voltage and current in the battery-charging circuit.

         (1) Primary circuit. The primary circuit of the transformer con-
                sists of two 115-volt windings which may be connected for
                 operation either from a 115- or a 230-volt a-c source (par.
                 10a).

    (2) Secondary circuit. The secondary circuit of the transformer is

    a single winding with 11 coarse adjustment taps on one end

    and 11 fine adjustment taps on the other end for control of

    the voltage and current delivered to the rectifier stacks,

    b. CIRCUIT BREAKERS. The circuit breakers close and open the a-c
supply circuit and the d-c input circuits, effectively turning the RA—

J1—B on and off.    They provide protection against overloads and short
circuits.

    c. RECTIFIER Stacks. Rectifier RA-91-B has 2 metallic selenium
stacks, each containing 20 selenium plates mounted on 1 bolt. Each

stack has three terminals (fig. 6). The outer taps are for the a-c in-

put; the center taps are for the d-c output. The two stacks are con-

nected in parallel. The function of the rectifier is to convert ac into
pulsating de.

    d. FILTER REACTOR. The filter reactor is used to filter the pulsat-
ing d-c output, thereby reducing noise in the telephone circuit into
which the batteries being charged are connected. The filter reactor

and the ammeter are connected in series with the battery-charging

circuit of the panel to which the RA-91-B is connected.

rectifier stack to the + terminal, through the filter reactor, through
the battery-charging circuit, through K2, through the ammeter to
the — terminal of the rectifier, and through the rectifier stack to
FINE tap 9. Note that the current always flows through the battery-
charging circuit in the same direction, regardless of the direction of
current flow through the secondary winding.
Note. FINE and COARSE taps 9 have been chosen as a typical example. The
circuit action is the same regardless of switch setting.
d. OVERLOAD-CONTROL CIRCUIT (par. 11). If the values of current
in either the input or output circuits exceed the operating limits of the
circuit breakers, the circuit breaker coils develop magnetic fields
strong enough to energize a solenoid. Operation of the solenoid opens
the appropriate circuit. A manual resetting mechanism closes the
circuit breaker and the appropriate circuit.

        Section 1. SHIPMENT AND LIMITED STORAGE

37. Packing Equipment for Shipment

    a. Prepare a wooden base slightly larger than the dimensions of the
base of Rectifier RA—91-B.

    6. Disconnect the input and output cords.  Clean the unit and
place it on the wooden base.

    c. Attach the equipment to the base with metal straps or other
means. Place desiccant around the rectifier base and wrap the entire
unit with flexible corrugated paper.

    d. Place the technical manual and cords in a close-fitting cardboard
container.

    e. Inclose both the cardboard container and the corrugated-paper-
wrapped unit with a moisture-vaporproof barrier and place them in-
side another corrugated-paper wrapping. Inclose this in a waterproof

liner.

   f. Use the wooden base as a foundation; build up the sides and the
top of a wooden inclosure and nail securely.

g. Bind the wooden box with metal straps to protect the equipment

against rough handling.

38. Packing for Limited Storage

    a. Clean and dry the equipment thoroughly.
    b. Place the equipment in a moisture-vaporproof barrier with a
quantity of desiccant placed around the base. Heat-seal the barrier.
    c. Place the inclosed unit in a cool, dry, place.

Section Il. DEMOLITION TO PREVENT ENEMY USE

39. Methods of Demolition

    a. Smash. Use sledges, axes, handaxes, pickaxes, hammers, crow-
bars, and heavy tools.

    b. Cut. Use axes, handaxes, and machetes.

23. Scope of Maintenance

   The maintenance duties assigned to the operator of Rectifier RA-
91-C are listed below together with a reference to the paragraphs
covering the specific maintenance function. The duties assigned do
not require tools or test equipment other than those issued with the
telephone central office equipment of which the rectifier is a part.

   a. Daily preventive maintenance checks and services (par. 27).
   b. Monthly preventive maintenance checks and services (par. 27.1).

   c. Adjustment of ammeter (par. 16).

24. Tools Required for Maintenance

   The tools required for maintenance are the same as those issued
with the telephone central office equipment of which the rectifier 1s a

part.

25. Preventive Maintenance

   Preventive maintenance is the systematic care, servicing, and in-
spection of equipment to prevent the occurrence of trouble, to reduce
downtime, and to assure that the equipment is serviceable.

   a. Systematic Care. The procedures given in pargraphs 27 and

27.1 cover routine systematic care and cleaning essential to proper
upkeep and operation of the equipment.

   b. Preventive Maintenance Checks and Services. The preventive
maintenance checks and services charts (pars. 27 and 27.1) outline
functions to be performed at specific intervals. These checks and
services are to maintain Army equipment in a combat serviceable con-
dition; that is, in good general (physical) condition and in good
operating condition. To assist operators in maintaining combat serv-

iceability, the charts indicate what to check, how to check, and what the
normal conditions are; the references column lists illustrations, para-
graphs, or manuals that contain supplementary information. If the
defect cannot be remedied by the operator, higher echelon maintenance

or repair is required. Records and reports of these checks and serv-
 ices must be made in accordance with the requirements set forth in

 TM 38-750.

   Columns are as follows:

initial operation of the equipment. Under “Running Spare Items” the
quantities listed are those initially with the equipment as spare parts.

Chapter 1
                           INTRODUCTION
                                                                           PURPOSE

Your Trainer's Guide gives you , the unit commander , the training manager, and the trainer, some of the
information you need to help plan , conduct, and evaluate unit training. Unit training is the most important
job of military leaders .
The training manager and the trainer must do their jobs right . When soldiers are trained , they do their jobs
right and in turn complete the unit's mission . Your Trainer's Guide can help you tailor training to the specific
needs of the soldiers and to evaluate that training . It lists the tasks that MOS 24K skill level 1 , 2, and 3 soldiers
must know , where the tasks are taught , and the basic information needed to set up individual training. Use
this Trainer's Guide along with the Soldier's Manual , FM 9-24K , Improved HAWK Continuous Wave
Radar Repairer; TC 9-24K 1/2 ( JB ) , Improved HAWK Continuous Wave Radar Repairer ; FM 21-2,
Soldier's Manual of Common Tasks , Skill Level 1 ; and FM 21-2 , Soldier's Manual of Common Tasks , Skill
Levels 2, 3 , and 4.

                                                COMMANDER'S RESPONSIBILITY
The commander is responsible for the unit mission . Soldier's can't do their job if they haven't learned how .
For the unit to succeed you must insure each soldier is satisfactorily trained . This Trainer's Guide and the
Trainer's Guides for FM 21-2 and FM 21-3 give you , your training manager, and your trainers some of the
information needed to plan , conduct, and evaluate training for MOS 24K .

                                       TRAINING MANAGER'S RESPONSIBILITY
You are responsible for planning, evaluating, monitoring, and providing the resources for training. The
Improved HAWK Continuous Wave Radar Repairer must learn and become proficient in several tasks . The
tasks in FM 9-24K Soldier's Manual, are listed in chapters 2 and 3 and cover some of the following

                       Land Navigation

                  I Transportation
                       Leadership
                       First Aid

                       Weapon Proficiency
                       Vehicles and Equipment
                      Maintenance and Repair

                       Supply
Skill Level 1 and 2 tasks selected from FM 9-24K , FM 21-2, and FM 21-3 are listed in TC 9-24K1/ 2 (JB ).
Each Soldier's Manual tells you and the soldier:

                       What TASKS the soldier is expected to perform .
                  1 Under what CONDITION the task is done .
                  I The STANDARD the soldier must reach to do the tasks.
                  1 The PERFORMANCE MEASURES needed to complete the task .

                  I What REFERENCES are needed to help do the task .
You must make a training plan trainers can use ; provide for the resources ( building, room, area , equipment ,
supplies , funds, training aids and devices, and so forth ); monitor the training to make sure it meets the needs
of the soldiers ; and evaluate the training when it is completed .

                                                      TRAINER'S RESPONSIBILITY
You must prepare, conduct , and critique training. Use the training plan to find out what must be taught ,
where , when , and to whom . When planning training , don't rely solely on your own knowledge of the subject.
Review reference materials to insure you use the latest information. Use TC 21-5-7 , Training Management in
Battalions, and FM 21-6, How to Prepare and Conduct Military Training, for basic guidance .

                                               INDIVIDUAL TRAINING PROGRAM

For each soldier to be trained , you need an individual training program to plan , train , and evaluate training.
Use the task list for MOS 24K in chapters 2 and 3 of the Trainer's Guide to make your individual training
plans. Each task is listed ( task number, task title, primary references for the task , the skill level of training , and
where the training for the task is done) . A letter in a column shows where the training is done , formal training
in a school , or unit training. The letter "Q " shows where QUALIFICATION training is done , the letter " A "
shows where ADDITIONAL training is done . The task list shows those tasks taught in Advanced Individual
Training and the tasks taught in the unit through supervised on -the - job training , scheduled training, and self
study .

 At appendix A of the Trainer's Guide is a consolidated list of references for the tasks ; appendix B is the job
 description for MOS 24K .

TRAIN AND RETRAIN

Skills not practiced are soon lost . Athletics is a good example of why training and retraining is needed .
Athletes must practice to retain their skills . The soldier is no different. For athletes , the coach sees to it that all
team members are trained in their tasks . You , like the coach, must see to it that your team is trained to do its
job . As a trainer you must verify, after a proper amount of time , that soldiers can do their job . Feedback from
supervisors using Job Books will help . Design your training program to correct areas that have been
determined through performance evaluation as needing improvement . You can get more feedback from the
SQT unit profile summary sheet . It shows the number and percentage of soldiers failing each task . Continued
evaluation will give you information for the Individual Training Program .

                        ENLISTED PERSONNEL MANAGEMENT SYSTEM ( EPMS )

Management System . It affects the training, evaluation , classification , and promotion of every enlisted
soldier in the Army . The system is designed to provide career incentives , progression , and professionalism ,
while providing qualified people to carry out the Army's mission .

                                              SKILL QUALIFICATION TEST ( SQT )

The skill level 1 soldier is evaluated by the SQT. The skill level 2 and 3 soldier is evaluated by the SQT and
senior enlisted evaluation report ( SEER) . Soldiers are then classified and considered for promotion . The skill
qualification test is a yearly test designed to test the soldier's ability to perform the tasks at the skill level held
and the lower skill levels in the MOS .
 SQT PARTS

 The SQT is based on the tasks in FM 9-24K , FM 21-2, and FM 21-3, and may consist of three parts .

                   I SKILL COMPONENT . This part tests the soldier's knowledge of those
                       tasks that cannot easily be tested by the hands-on method .

                   IHANDS-ON COMPONENT. This part tests the soldier's ability to perform
                       selected tasks on the TOE or TDA equipment .

                        NOTE : ALTERNATE HANDS- ON COMPONENT . This part is a written
                       test to be used when it is not practical to administer the hands-on component .

                   I JOB SITE COMPONENT. This part consists of tasks that are not suited for
                       skilled qualification testing for either the skill or hands-on components .
                        They are long or complex tasks . You or your designated representative certify
                        the soldier has done the tasks satisfactorily on the job .

SQT NOTICE

 Soldiers receive their SQT notice and Job Site Component Booklet 90 days before the SQT. Each notice lists
 the tasks in the SQT . They are selected from FM 9-24K , FM 21-2, and FM 21-3 . The first SQT a soldier takes
 ( SQT 1 , SQT 2 and so on) depends on the pay grade held . Soldiers are tested once a year in their primary
 MOS . To successfully prepare for the SQT, your soldiers need the above manuals.

 SQT SCORE
 The SQT score is used to verify the soldier's present skill level or to make the soldier eligible to be considered
for promotion .

                       If the soldier makes a score of 60 percent or more it is proof of proficiency in the
                        MOS at the present skill level . The soldier verifies the skill level held .
                        If a soldier doesn't pass the test , the soldier is not qualified at that skill level. The test
                   must be taken again . Another failure, and the soldier becomes subject to
                  reclassification .

SQT FEEDBACK
About 30 days after the SQT, you get an SQT unit profile summary sheet that shows the number and
percentage of soldiers in your unit who pass or fail the test . Each soldier also receives a notice of test results
with a list of tasks failed , if any . You get a copy of each soldier's test results to evaluate the strengths and
weaknesses of individual skills .

                   ARMY TRAINING AND EVALUATION PROGRAM ( ARTEP)
The ARTEP lists the collective tasks that must be performed by the unit under given conditions and to given
standards . The association between the individual tasks of the Soldier's Manuals and the collective tasks of
the ARTEP is a critical relationship. The collective tasks of the ARTEP are composed of the individual tasks
identified in the Soldier's Manuals. The ability of your unit to perform collective tasks is based on each
soldier's mastery of individual tasks. ARTEP 9-58 , Perishing and HAWK / NIKE General Support Missile
Maintenance Companies, is one of the best tools you have for training and evaluating your unit . As a training
tool use it to train soldiers to work together to do mission tasks and use it to evaluate their training . The
ARTEP gives you :

                  I a list of selected tasks in your unit's TOE mission ,
                  I conditions under which the tasks must be performed,
                  I standards that define the acceptable performance of the tasks, and

                      a system for using performance -oriented training to help in developing an effective
                      training program .
FEEDBACK
The SQT gives you feedback on individuals and the ARTEP gives you feedback on your unit . This feedback
must be supplemented by your evaluation of your unit's level of performance. You must be able to track your
soldier's and your unit's progress through the necessary training that leads to mission success. Unannounced
testing is the best way to determine the level of proficiency of your unit . Additional feedback on the state of
training for each soldier includes the use of administrative data and files to determine turnover, institutional
training background , and experience levels . For skill level 1 and 2 soldiers, the Job Book is a valuable source
that identifies training deficiencies.
Once you have identified areas that need additional emphasis , use the Soldier's Manual and the ARTEP to
structure your training. You must train your soldiers to do the tasks in their present skill level and then train
them to do the tasks in the next higher skill level.
Evaluate each individual soldier's ability to do the tasks in Soldier's Manuals . Evaluate the unit's ability to do
its ARTEP missions. Feedback from the SQT and the ARTEP can provide a solid foundation on which you
can build a successful training program .

                                                           ADDITIONAL TRAINING
Additional training upgrades the effectiveness of your personnel, and thus , your unit . You can use service
school training , installation school training , and unit training for any training needed to qualify soldiers in
their skill level and prepare them for duty at the next skill level .
Prime considerations for training are time , training areas, and available assets . Training away from the unit
has much appeal, but time , facilities, or funds limit or prohibit use of major training areas , more local training
should be used . Imaginative trainers and maximum use of available training aids and devices can make the
most of available assets . There are many valuable tools to help improve the unit's performance. Integration of
training with the individual tasks in the Soldier's Manual and with the collective tasks in the ARTEP is a good
start for a comprehensive training plan .

                                                                      Appendix B
                                                                JOB DESCRIPTION
                                             Improved HAWK Continuous Wave

                                                                    Radar Repairer
                                                                  ( IH CW Rdr Rep )

                                                                          MOS 24K

                                                                          SUMMARY

Supervises or performs direct and general support maintenance on Improved HAWK continuous wave
radars and associated equipment .

                                                                              DUTIES
MOSC_24K 10 : Performs support maintenance on electronic systems of Improved Continuous Wave
Acquisition Radar (ICWAR) and Improved High Power Illuminator Radar (IHIPIR) ; operates and
performs organizational maintenance on Support Maintenance Test Equipment (SMTE) Shops 2 and 3 ,
Mobile Team Shop (MTS) , and system associated test equipment employed in maintenance of the ICWAR
and IHIPIR ; and performs support maintenance , as authorized , on associated system peculiar portable test
equipment . EQUIPMENT MAINTENANCE . Tests and adjusts components to specific tolerances and
determines shortcomings and malfunctions in electronic assemblies , subassemblies, modules, and circuit
elements with common and system special-design test equipment . Repairs unserviceable items by removing
and replacing defective components and parts. Determines serviceability and disposition of defective
assemblies , subassemblies , modules , and circuit elements . INSPECTION . Performs quality control
measures . Serves on inspection teams .
MOSC 24K 20 : Performs duties shown in preceding level of skill and provides technical guidance to lower
grade personnel . INSPECTION . Performs initial and final type checkout and inspection of designated
system items and their assemblies and sub-assemblies . EQUIPMENT MAINTENANCE . Installs equipment
modifications. Provides technical assistance to supported units . Completets maintenance and supply forms.
MOSC 24K30: Supervises duties shown in preceding levels of skill . Establishes workloads and repair
priorities . SUPERVISION . Recommends procedures for receipt , storage , inspection , testing and repair of
items . Implements quality control measures. Supervises inspection and maintenance teams . Organizes and
conducts on -the -job training programs . Determines faulty work practices and demonstrates proper
maintenance and troubleshooting techniques . Establishes and keeps maintenance records. Prepares
maintenance reports.
